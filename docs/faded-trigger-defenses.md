# Faded triggers against prediction-shift detection, a literature memo

PSBD-TM is evaded when the attacker fades the trigger at test time. The backdoor still fires at a lower blend weight, patch opacity or warp strength, but the triggered decision carries less surplus and the score moves into the clean range before attack success falls. This memo reads the dose records under `results/_experiments/evidence_surplus/trigger_dose/` again, states what the evidence-surplus account (`docs/evidence-surplus-theory.md`) says the window should look like, surveys how the literature handles weak, faded and partial triggers. It then ranks what this project could do at inference time and at training time. The reader is assumed to know PSBD-TM, the dose experiment and the theory note.

Written on 2026-09-30 on branch `rewrite` at commit `2473938`. The dose numbers come from the 16 JSON records named above (256 pairs per model, 3 passes, the canonical adaptive rate and clean-validation thresholds). The clean critical-rate quantiles and the class makeup of the clean tail were computed for this memo on CPU from the stage-1 caches under `results/<folder>/psbd/before_attention_norm_token_mask/` with `defenses.decision.load_critical_rate_from_disk`. Every paper was read in full text where the claim quoted depends on a number, and the rest are marked as read from the abstract. The memo has 15 sections. They cover the measured window and its evidence-surplus reading, 9 literature families, the contradictions and missing controls and the ranked ideas for inference and for training, with the references at the end.

## The measured window

The dose records split into 2 regimes, and the caller's 2 examples come from different ones. **Collapse regime.** On `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1` and `vit_cifar10_bpp_0_05` the hit AUROC falls with the dose while ASR holds, and at Blend 0.6 it is 0.517 with ASR 0.934. There the per-input signal is gone, and the faded triggered images have a median fractional PSU of 0.872 against 0.879 for the clean twins at dose 0. CIFAR-10 WaNet sits in this regime at every dose, full strength included (hit AUROC 0.434 at dose 1). This is the case the theory note's magnitude route predicts, and it is the only case where a per-input probe has nothing left to read.

**Gate regime.** On BadNets, LF, CIFAR-100 Blend and Tiny WaNet the hit AUROC stays between 0.88 and 0.99 while ASR falls, which is the gate the theory note predicts for patch triggers: a carrier that still fires still renormalizes. Detection falls with the dose here mainly because fewer images fire, and among those that do, TPR at 10% FPR stays at 0.73 or more. The loss at 1% FPR is real, though: CIFAR-10 BadNets at dose 0.5 keeps a hit TPR of 0.59 against 0.90 at full dose, and GTSRB LF at 0.5 keeps 0.51 against 0.87. The caller's "weak but not gone" description fits this regime and not CIFAR-10 Blend.

**The 1% FPR threshold sits inside a clean tail.** On CIFAR-10 and GTSRB between 4.5% and 8.4% of the 2000 clean validation images never change their majority answer at any cached masking rate up to 0.9, against 0.3% to 0.5% on CIFAR-100 and Tiny. On each of the 5 CIFAR-10 and GTSRB models checked, that tail is 1 non-target class almost entirely (all 144 tail images of `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1` are predicted class 4, all 90 of `vit_cifar10_bpp_0_05` class 7, all 160 of `vit_cifar10_wanet_0_1` class 2, 145 of 168 of `vit_cifar10_badnet_a2o_0_01` class 6, all 108 of `vit_gtsrb_lf_0_01` class 13). None of it is predicted as the target class 0. This is the modal perturbation attractor of Q34 in `docs/open-questions.md`, the class masking pushes inputs toward, whose own images cannot be moved by masking. The 1% quantile of pooled clean scores is therefore set by attractor-class images, which is why the window opens at 1% FPR on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB and barely on CIFAR-100 (`vit_cifar100_blend_0_1` keeps a hit TPR of 0.88 at 1% FPR at dose 0.6).

**TaCT is a threshold problem at every dose.** `vit_cifar10_tact_0_01` reads a twin AUROC of 0.978 and a TPR of 0.004 at full dose, and `vit_gtsrb_tact_0_05` reads 0.962 and 0.012 at 1% FPR. The triggered TaCT images are more stable than their source-class twins (median PSU 0.903 against about 0.995) and much less stable than the pooled clean threshold (0.39 on CIFAR-10 Blend's model at 1%, for scale). The twin pairing compares within the source class, whose clean images are fragile, so the AUROC overstates what a thresholded detector achieves on these models, and fading adds nothing to a TPR that is already near 0.

## An evidence-surplus reading of the window

**Result.** In the additive form the ratio of the dose at which the detector stops flagging to the dose at which the backdoor stops firing is at most the clean surplus at the threshold's quantile. No probe that reads surplus along the same axis can close a window of that width, because inside it a faded triggered input is by construction as surplus-poor as the clean image at the threshold. The gate form closes it for patch triggers, and the 1% FPR threshold on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB sits in a censored clean tail, which makes the bound loose there.

The theory note's additive form writes the surplus ratio as the evidence carried over the evidence needed, with the critical removal fraction $p^* = 1 - 1/S$. Fading the trigger scales only the trigger's contributions.

$$S_x(a) = \frac{E_C + a\,E_T}{-b}, \qquad \text{fires} \iff S_x(a) > 1, \qquad \text{flagged at FPR } q \iff p^*_x(a) > p^*_{(1-q)} \iff S_x(a) > S_q = \frac{1}{1 - p^*_{(1-q)}}$$

$$a_{\text{fire}} = \frac{-b - E_C}{E_T}, \qquad a_{\text{det}} = \frac{-b\,S_q - E_C}{E_T}, \qquad \frac{a_{\text{det}}}{a_{\text{fire}}} = \frac{S_q + k}{1 + k} \leq S_q, \qquad k = \frac{-E_C}{-b} \geq 0$$

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| $a$ | the dose, 1 for the trained trigger and 0 for the clean image |
| $E_T$ | the trigger's summed contribution to the target's margin over the runner-up at full dose |
| $E_C$ | the image content's summed contribution to the same margin, at most 0 for an image whose true class is not the target |
| $b$ | the null margin of the triggered image, negative, as in the theory note |
| $S_x(a)$ | the surplus ratio of the faded triggered image |
| $p^*_{(1-q)}$ | the $(1-q)$ quantile of the clean critical removal fraction, the clean image at the threshold |
| $S_q$ | the clean surplus at that quantile |
| $a_{\text{fire}}$, $a_{\text{det}}$ | the doses below which the backdoor stops firing and the detector stops flagging |
| $k$ | how strongly the content opposes the target, in units of the null margin |

The derivation takes the fractional PSU at a fixed rate as monotone in $p^*$, which the critical-rate account supports at a Spearman of 0.883 across placements, and it takes $b$ equal for the faded image and the clean image at the threshold. Both are approximations and are flagged here. The bound is reached when the content carries no evidence against the target and shrinks toward 1 when it carries a lot. 3 consequences follow and each is testable.

1. The window widens as the FPR tightens, since $S_q$ grows with the quantile. From the caches, $S_q$ on `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1` is 3.33 at 10% FPR and unbounded at 1% (over 5% of clean images never flip up to rate 0.9), and on `vit_cifar100_blend_0_1` it is 2.0 at 10% and 5.0 at 1%. The measured CIFAR-10 Blend ratio, read from where ASR crosses 0.5 (dose about 0.45) and where the hit TPR crosses 0.5 (about 0.8 at 10% FPR, about 0.95 at 1% FPR), is about 1.8 and 2.1. Both lie under the bound, which is consistent and not yet a sharp test.
2. Training that makes the trigger stronger moves $a_{\text{fire}}$ and $a_{\text{det}}$ down together and leaves the ratio alone in the additive form. So SAM, a higher poison rate or longer training should shift the window in absolute dose without narrowing it, which is a prediction against the reading that a stronger backdoor is a safer one for the defender.
3. The gate form escapes the bound. When the trigger's evidence reaches the class token through a few late reads that each suffice, fading changes whether each read fires. A read that fires still renormalizes, so $p^*$ of a hit stays near the full-dose value until the gate closes. That is what the BadNets, LF and Tiny WaNet records show.

The bound says where a remedy has to come from. A remedy must either read something other than surplus that still tracks whether the backdoor fired, raise the faded input's surplus before probing (amplification), remove the attractor tail from the target's reference (calibration), move the backdoor from the additive to the gate form (training), or make the faded attack fail instead of detecting it (purification on a different axis). A purifier on the same axis cannot help much, since an input that evades detection has a clean-like $p^*$ and a purifier that keeps clean accuracy keeps it too. The families below are read against these routes.

## Direct precedents on test-time trigger intensity

**Training-inference trigger intensity (TITIM).** Lin et al. ([USENIX Security 2025](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/lin-chenhao)) train ResNet-18 backdoors at 1 intensity and attack at another, for BadNets opacity and size, Blend, SIG, BppAttack, a compression trigger, WaNet and a style trigger, with an appendix on CNNs and 3 ViT variants (SimpleViT, CvT, T2T-ViT). 2 findings matter here. Lowering the inference intensity below the training intensity evades input detection before it stops the attack: a BadNets square trained at opacity 1.0 and attacked at 0.7 keeps ASR 91.62% (against 99.65%) while STRIP's AUC falls from 0.99 to 0.80 and SCALE-UP's from 0.96 to 0.62 (their Figure 14). Training on mixed intensities (opacities 1.0 and 0.1) raises the worst-case ASR over test opacities from 10.61% to 92.77%, so an attacker can buy a backdoor that fires across a wide dose range with no term that mentions a defense. They also report that Februus purification leaves about 50% ASR at opacity 0.5, that RAB smoothing removes weak-training backdoors and fails at high training intensity, and that Neural Cleanse reverses a trigger even when the model was trained at an intensity too low to fire. Their only defensive suggestion is multi-stage defense, left to future work. For this project it is the published version of experiment B on ConvNets. It also fixes the threat model: an intensity mismatch is a non-adaptive attack in the sense of the mentor's request, since no loss term references PSBD. What would confirm that PSBD-TM behaves like STRIP and SCALE-UP here is the window we already measured. What would distinguish it is the gate regime, which their input detectors do not show on patch triggers (STRIP falls on BadNets square).

**Low-ASR backdoors.** Riaz and Yu ([arXiv 2608.27288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27288), read from the HTML) weaken a trained backdoor by reverse training, applying the trigger to clean images with their true labels, and show that Neural Cleanse, STRIP, FreeEagle and DeBackdoor fail on the resulting low-ASR models. Their threat model has the attacker keep a local copy and pick the inputs that still fire, which is the per-input version of fading: the attacker submits each input at the lowest dose that fires on it. On MNIST, CIFAR-10 and GTSRB ConvNets only. For this project it names the worst case the dose experiment does not yet measure, a per-input minimal dose, where the additive form puts every hit at $S_x \approx 1$ and $p^* \approx 0$. That case is the easiest for a 2-sided rule (below) and the hardest for the current 1-sided one.

**Weak backdoors in poisoned-sample detection.** Zhang et al. ([arXiv 2411.11525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11525), read from the LaTeX source in `literature/`) find that training-set detectors become unstable against weak backdoors (poison rate 1% and blend ratio 0.1 in their motivating figure), tie detection to the strength of the backdoor effect measured by the top trigger-activation changes, and train with SAM to amplify that effect, reporting +34.38% TPR on average over the detectors without SAM. Their setting is poisoned-sample detection in the training set, where the weak trigger is also the training trigger. For this project the useful part is the positive correlation between backdoor effect and detectability, which is the evidence-surplus claim in other words, and the SAM checkpoints already on disk let us test whether SAM moves the dose window (idea b3).

**Adaptive-Blend and Adaptive-Patch.** Qi et al. ([ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13613)) train with a weakened trigger and attack with the full one: Adaptive-Blend plants 50% of a 4 by 4 grid of pieces at opacity 0.15 in training and the whole trigger at 0.2 at test time, and Adaptive-Patch plants 1 of 4 patches at 50% opacity in training and 2 fully opaque patches at test time, both with regularization samples that keep their true label. They state the reason as giving the test-time trigger enough power to overcome the regularization samples, and they pay in ASR (76.5% for Adaptive-Blend without defense). This is the reverse of fading, and under the theory it manufactures surplus at test time, since the full trigger carries about twice the pieces the model learned to need, an r-of-n decision with $S \approx 2$. The repository's `attacks/adaptive_blend.py` keeps the partial cells and uses 1 alpha (0.2) for both phases. For this project the checkpoints `vit_cifar10_adaptive_blend_*` and `vit_gtsrb_adaptive_blend_*` are the cheapest available test of whether partial-evidence training moves a global trigger toward the gate regime (idea b1).

**Adversarial boosting of a weak trigger.** Pang et al. ([CCS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01559), abstract) show that adversarial inputs and poisoned models reinforce each other, so an attacker can add a small adversarial perturbation to a faded trigger to restore firing. For this project it is the attacker's next step after fading, and it adds margin without adding surplus along the token axis, which the theory predicts PSBD-TM would not see. It is out of the mentor's non-adaptive scope when the perturbation is optimized against the detector and inside it when it only restores the target.

## Amplification at test time

**Pixel amplification (SCALE-UP).** Guo et al. ([ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03251)) multiply every pixel by 2 to 11 and flag inputs whose label survives, on the premise that a triggered prediction is scale-consistent. It amplifies image and trigger together and so does not undo fading. TITIM measured its AUC falling from 0.96 to 0.62 at opacity 0.7. The port is `detectors/scale_up.py`. For this project it is a baseline to run on the dose series, and the prediction is that it falls at least as early as PSBD-TM on Blend.

**Parameter amplification (IBD-PSC).** Hou et al. ([ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09786)) scale the BatchNorm parameters of the deepest layers and score the average confidence over amplified models. Their adaptive section varies the poison rate (0.02 to 0.1, AUROC above 0.98) and trains with an adaptive loss (WaNet AUROC 0.819 to 0.947), and it does not vary the test-time intensity (checked in the HTML full text). The repository's ViT port is `ibd_psc` and `ibd_psc_calibrated`, and the withdrawn `gain_scale` operator at `mlp_norm_out` is the same idea on LayerNorm gains. Under the theory, amplifying late gains scales the residual reads that carry the trigger and the clean content alike, which raises both surpluses and leaves the window ratio alone in the additive form. What would change that is amplification restricted to the heads or tokens that read the trigger, which requires knowing them.

**Residual amplification.** No paper was found that extrapolates an input away from its denoised version, $x + \lambda\,(x - D(x))$ with $D$ a blur or a denoiser, to restore a faded trigger before a consistency test. It is the input-space reading of "raise $a$ back toward 1" and it is this memo's proposal, marked as unpublished. It can only work for triggers that live in the part of the image $D$ removes: high-frequency Blend patterns, BPP quantization and LF only if $D$ removes low frequencies. Clean images are changed by the same extrapolation, so the test is whether the clean PSU distribution moves less than the triggered one, and nothing in the theory predicts that it will.

**Estimating the trigger from confident cases (matched filtering).** The detector does not have to raise the dose of the faded input if it can estimate the trigger's direction and look for it. Neural Cleanse (Wang et al., [IEEE S&P 2019](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~ravenben/publications/pdf/backdoor-sp19.pdf)) reverses a minimal trigger per class, and TITIM's Table 7 shows it reverses BadNets at 100% re-ASR even for models trained at opacity 0.2, where the original trigger fires on 9.75% of inputs. BaDExpert (Xie et al., [ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12439), abstract) extracts the backdoor functionality itself by fine-tuning on mislabeled clean images and reports AUROC 0.99 over 12 attacks, ViT included. Spectral signatures (Tran et al., [NeurIPS 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00636)) find the backdoor as the top singular direction of a class's centered representations. In this project the backdoor direction $u$ of experiment A (`experiments/evidence_surplus/surplus_factor/`) is already computed from triggered and clean pairs. A detector that scores $\langle h(x), u\rangle$ at the last block reads whether the input moved along $u$, and firing requires moving far enough to cross the head's boundary, so under the theory such a score tracks firing rather than surplus and has no window in the additive form. The defender must estimate $u$ without labels, from inputs PSBD-TM flags at 1% FPR at full dose or from the top singular direction of the queries predicted as the suspected target. The price is a threat model with a pool of queries or a few confident catches, and Qi et al.'s adaptive attacks exist precisely to shrink latent separability.

## Purification

**Inpainting and reconstruction.** Februus (Doan et al., [ACSAC 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03369)) locates the trigger with Grad-CAM and inpaints it, ZIP (Shi et al., [NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12175), abstract) blurs the image and restores it with a pretrained diffusion model, and BDMAE (Sun et al., [arXiv 2303.15564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15564), abstract) masks regions and restores them with a masked autoencoder, which is the closest to a ViT's own masking. TITIM found Februus leaves about 50% ASR at opacity 0.5, because a faded trigger is harder to localize. For this project these are purification defenses outside PSBD's detection framing, and ZIP's blur step is the relevant part: a low-amplitude high-frequency trigger is exactly what a blur removes first.

**Spatial transforms.** Li et al. ([arXiv 2004.04692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04692), abstract) show that flipping or shrinking a test image breaks many static triggers, and that attackers restore robustness by training on transformed triggers. A faded trigger has less margin to lose, so a transform that a full trigger survives may break it. This matters as a cheap purifier for the collapse regime, where detection has nothing to read.

**Randomized smoothing across axes.** Cohen et al. ([ICML 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02918)) certify an $\ell_2$ radius $\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(p_A)$ for a Gaussian-smoothed classifier, and Grosse et al. ([Computers and Security 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06721)) observe that at full strength the decision around a triggered input is smoother than around a clean one, which is why smoothing is a poor defense against full triggers. Fading shrinks the trigger's $\ell_2$ norm in proportion to the dose for Blend, LF, BPP and opacity-faded patches, so the faded trigger moves inside the radius a smoothed classifier certifies while clean content, whose amplitude does not shrink, stays put. This is the cross-axis argument of this memo: token masking removes the trigger's evidence and the content's evidence in the same proportion, while additive input noise drowns small-amplitude evidence first. It is an inference and no published result on faded triggers states it. It predicts the opposite of Grosse et al.'s observation only below the dose where the faded trigger's margin to noise becomes small.

**The same-axis limit.** A purifier that outputs the majority label over masked passes fails an input exactly when its $p^*$ is below the purification rate. The defender picks that rate at the $\varepsilon$ quantile of clean $p^*$ to lose at most $\varepsilon$ of clean accuracy, so the attack survives both detection and purification whenever $p^*_{(\varepsilon)} < p^*_x(a) \leq p^*_{(1-q)}$, which is the bulk of the clean distribution. On CIFAR-10 Blend at dose 0.6 the faded hits sit at the clean median, so same-axis purification is predicted to remove almost nothing there. It only removes the barely-firing inputs of a per-input minimal-dose attacker, which a 2-sided rule catches anyway.

## Pooled and sequential detection

**Query histories.** Chen et al. ([SPAI 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05587)) keep a history of past queries and flag the self-similar sequences that black-box adversarial search produces. The analogue here is that a faded-trigger attacker sends many inputs that share 1 trigger and 1 predicted class, so the defender can pool the scores of all queries predicted as the same class. Gao et al. ([ICML 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11415), abstract) show that a two-sample test with a deep kernel, tuned for power, detects batches of adversarial examples that a Gaussian-kernel MMD misses. Guan et al. ([CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06107), abstract) assume the defender sees a batch of partially poisoned test data and detect and repair from it. For this project the pooled test is a model-level alarm and does not decide which input is poisoned.

**Power of a pooled rank test.** A 1-sided Mann-Whitney test between $n$ pooled query scores and $m$ clean reference scores of the same predicted class has, under the normal approximation, $z = (A - \tfrac{1}{2})/\sqrt{(n+m+1)/(12nm)}$ with pooled AUROC $A = \tfrac{1}{2} + \pi\,(A_{\text{hit}} - \tfrac{1}{2})$, where $A_{\text{hit}}$ is the per-input hit AUROC and $\pi$ the share of attack queries among those predicted as the target. The counts below were computed from that expression for $z = 2.326$ (1% one-sided).

| $A_{\text{hit}}$ | $\pi$ | queries needed, $m = 200$ | queries needed, $m = 2000$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.52 (CIFAR-10 Blend, dose 0.6) | 1.0 | never | 2584 |
| 0.52 | 0.1 | never | never |
| 0.74 (CIFAR-10 BPP, dose 0.8) | 1.0 | 9 | 8 |
| 0.74 | 0.1 | never | 1287 |
| 0.94 (GTSRB LF, dose 0.5) | 1.0 | 3 | 3 |
| 0.94 | 0.1 | never | 264 |

2 things follow. Pooling rescues the gate regime from its 1% FPR problem with a handful of queries when the attack dominates the target-class traffic, and it cannot rescue the collapse regime at any realistic query count, which is what the surplus bound predicts. The limit is the clean reference: about 200 validation images per class on CIFAR-10 cap the power no matter how many queries arrive, so the reference size decides whether a mixed stream is detectable.

**Class-conditional calibration.** Conformal prediction with per-class calibration (Mondrian conformal, and the class-specific coverage of Sadinle et al., [JASA 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2017.1395341)) guarantees the error rate within each class separately. Applied here it sets the threshold for an input from the clean validation images predicted as the same class, so a triggered input predicted as the target is compared with target-class clean images and never with the attractor tail. Q34 in `docs/open-questions.md` refuted a different rule, dropping the attractor class from the pooled reference, which only loosened the threshold and raised the realized FPR by the same amount. The class-conditional rule keeps every class's FPR at $q$ and moves the budget between classes, so its effect differs. The attractor analysis above says where it should help: on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB at 1% FPR, and not on CIFAR-100 or Tiny. The repository already has a class-conditional 2-sided PSU from the all-to-all study (`experiments/all_to_all_detection/README.md`), which reads 0.631 there, so the code path exists. The cost is a noisy threshold: 200 images per class put the 1% quantile on the 2nd lowest score, and the finite-sample correction $\lceil (n_c+1)q \rceil$ must be used. An attacker who picks the attractor class as target would face a reference that never flips, which is a risk to state and measure.

## Multi-rate and multi-pass scoring

**Severity sweeps.** TeCo (Liu et al., [CVPR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18191)) applies 15 corruptions at 5 severities and scores the spread of the severity at which each corruption changes the label, so its score is a multi-rate statistic by construction. IBD-PSC sweeps the number of amplified layers. The project's own ladder already gives the per-sample critical rate $p^*$ on 10 cached rates. The theory note reads PSBD at any placement as a 2-sample comparison of $p^*$, and a single-rate PSU with 3 passes is a noisy proxy for it.

**What the theory predicts for faded triggers.** A rate-free $p^*$ score and more passes both reduce estimation noise, so they can only raise the hit AUROC to the level the $p^*$ distributions allow. Where the faded hits and the clean images share a $p^*$ distribution, as at CIFAR-10 Blend dose 0.6 where the median PSUs agree to 0.007, no rate and no pass count separates them. The prediction is a small gain at doses where the hit AUROC is between 0.7 and 0.9 (CIFAR-10 Blend 0.8 and BPP 0.8) and none at 0.6. A gain at 0.6 would refute the claim that $p^*$ overlap is the limit.

## Certified ablation bounds

**Patch certificates.** Levine and Feizi ([NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/47ce0875420b2dbacfc5535f94e68433-Abstract.html)) certify against patches by classifying every column or block ablation and voting, PatchCleanser (Xiang et al., [USENIX Security 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09135)) uses 2 rounds of masks so that 1 mask covers the patch wherever it is and the second round filters malicious 1-mask predictions, ViP (Li et al., [ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/7173_ECCV_2022_paper.php), abstract) does certified detection and recovery with an MAE-pretrained ViT, and Salman et al. ([CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Salman_Certified_Patch_Robustness_via_Smoothed_Vision_Transformers_CVPR_2022_paper.html)) show that ViTs tolerate ablation well when masked tokens are dropped. A patch certificate does not depend on opacity, so it covers faded BadNets and TaCT patches up to the certified size. For this project it applies only to the patch attacks, which are the ones already in the gate regime, and our models were not fine-tuned on ablated inputs, so clean accuracy under double masking is the first thing to measure. It does not apply to Blend, BPP, LF or WaNet, which cover every token.

## Published detectors against weak triggers

The table states what is published on weak or faded test-time triggers for each input-level detector the project ports or cites, and what the port lets us measure on the dose series. Every detector in `detectors.DETECTOR_NAMES` scores arbitrary inputs through `build_detector`, so all of them can run on faded images with no change to the ports.

| detector | published evidence on weak triggers | predicted behavior on our dose series |
|---|---|---|
| STRIP (Gao et al., [ACSAC 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06531)) | AUC 0.99 to 0.80 at opacity 0.7 (TITIM), fails on low-ASR models (Riaz and Yu) | falls with the dose on every attack, earlier than PSBD-TM on BadNets since superposition has no renormalization |
| SCALE-UP (Guo et al.) | AUC 0.96 to 0.62 at opacity 0.7 (TITIM) | falls with the dose, no gate regime |
| TeCo (Liu et al.) | none found on test-time intensity | the transition-severity spread shrinks with the dose, possibly slower on Blend since noise corruptions hit the faded pattern first |
| IBD-PSC (Hou et al.) | poison rate and adaptive loss only, no intensity sweep | falls with the dose while the calibrated variant keeps its FPR |
| BaDExpert (Xie et al.) | 12 attacks at 99% AUROC, ViT included (abstract), no intensity sweep found | the backdoor expert fires only when the backdoor fires, so it should track ASR. Not ported |
| TED and TED-LaST (Mo et al., [IEEE S&P 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02673), [arXiv 2506.10722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10722)) | TED-LaST adds label-supervised dynamics against Adaptive-Blend and Adaptive-Patch (abstract) | the nearest-neighbor rank trajectory of a faded hit should stay target-like in late layers, so TED may track firing |
| Beatrix (Ma et al., [NDSS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11715)) | none found | class-conditional Gram statistics of late features should track firing like the matched filter |
| Cognitive distillation (Huang et al., [ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10908)) | none found | the distilled mask of a faded Blend should grow toward the clean size, so it falls with the dose |

The pattern the table predicts is a split between detectors that read perturbation stability (STRIP, SCALE-UP, IBD-PSC, PSBD-TM) and detectors that read the latent footprint of firing (BaDExpert, TED, Beatrix, a matched filter). The first group should share PSBD-TM's window and the second should not, which is a single experiment with ports that already exist.

## Training-time levers

**Isolation and decoupling.** Anti-Backdoor Learning (Li et al., [NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11571)) isolates the images whose loss falls fastest and unlearns them, and TITIM measured its recall falling from 94.60% to 12.90% when the training trigger is weak. Decoupling (Huang et al., [ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03423)) trains the backbone self-supervised and the head supervised, so poisoned images do not cluster. Both remove or weaken the backdoor, which makes fading moot rather than detectable, and both change the model the detector reads. They are relevant only if the paper claims a pipeline, and the theory predicts that a partially removed backdoor is a low-surplus backdoor, so PSBD would be weakest exactly on what they leave behind.

**Strong augmentation.** Borgnia et al. ([ICASSP 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09527), abstract) find that mixup and CutMix mitigate backdoors without an accuracy cost. CutMix shows the model triggered images with a region replaced, so a global trigger is learned from fragments, which is what Adaptive-Blend's partial cells do on purpose. Under the theory, training on fragments turns an additive global trigger into an r-of-n or disjunctive one, whose faded hits keep their $p^*$ as long as each fragment's read fires. That moves Blend from the collapse regime to the gate regime and is the only training lever found that changes the aggregation form rather than the magnitude.

**Label smoothing and under-confidence.** Peng et al. ([arXiv 2202.11203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11203)) relabel poisoned images to the target with a probability below 1 and get under-confident backdoors that evade several defenses. Label smoothing caps the logit gap for clean and triggered images alike. The theory note already plans R5 (GTSRB BadNets, label smoothing 0.1) and R8 (benign control), and predicts that PSBD-TM on BadNets is unaffected because the renormalization route does not depend on the margin. For faded triggers the additive form adds a prediction: on Blend, capping the margin lowers $E_T$ relative to $-b$, which raises $a_{\text{fire}}$ and $a_{\text{det}}$ together, so the window stays at most $S_q$ wide in ratio and moves toward full dose. Q35 in `docs/open-questions.md` found that low confidence does not evade the fractional statistic at full dose, which is a separate question from the dose window.

**Sharpness-aware minimization.** Zhang et al. report that SAM training amplifies the backdoor effect and rescues detection of weak backdoors in the training set. Under the theory SAM raises the triggered surplus at full dose, so $a_{\text{fire}}$ and $a_{\text{det}}$ fall together, and the prediction is a shifted window of unchanged ratio. The checkpoints `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1_sam_rho_*` beside `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1` and its seeds 1 and 2 make this a 10 second test per model.

**Mixed-intensity poisoning by the attacker.** TITIM's mixing is the training-time form of the threat, and it is non-adaptive. The theory predicts that mixing at opacities 1.0 and 0.1 lowers $a_{\text{fire}}$ by a factor of up to 10 on Blend, so the backdoor fires at doses where the trigger is nearly invisible, and that the ratio $a_{\text{det}}/a_{\text{fire}}$ stays bounded by $S_q$. If the ratio grows instead, the additive form is wrong for mixed training. The repository has the training-intensity axis for WaNet already (`vit_cifar10_wanet_0_01_trig_s{1,2,4}`, `vit_gtsrb_wanet_0_01_trig_s{1,2,4}`, strengths 1, 2 and 4), which is TITIM's other half.

## The attacker's stealth and success trade-off

TITIM's curves are the published trade-off: ASR against inference intensity rises and saturates while detector AUC falls earlier. The gap between them is the attacker's operating band. Qi et al. pay the other way, giving up ASR (76.5%) for latent inseparability, and Riaz and Yu give up ASR on purpose and select inputs that still fire. The right single number for this project is the evasive success, the share of faded inputs that reach the target and pass the detector, since ASR and TPR reported separately hide the band. From the table above the worst ViT case outside TaCT and CIFAR-10 WaNet is CIFAR-10 Blend at dose 0.6, where 83% of faded inputs reach the target and pass at 1% FPR, against 43% at full dose. The maximum over doses of the evasive success per model and per FPR gives the attacker's best fixed dose. The per-input minimal dose of Riaz and Yu's threat model gives an upper bound on it.

## Contradictions and missing controls

1. **The hit-only AUROC has no 0.5 null.** The benign CIFAR-10 probes read a hit AUROC of 0.61 to 0.82 with ASR between 0.008 and 0.145 (0.824 for the Blend probe at dose 1), so selecting images the trigger sends to the target selects images that score lower than their twins without any backdoor. A hit AUROC of 0.9 on a faded trigger is therefore weaker evidence than it looks, and the control is a comparison with clean validation images predicted as the target class.
2. **TaCT's twin AUROC does not predict its TPR.** A twin AUROC of 0.978 with a TPR of 0.004 at 1% FPR means the pairing compares within a fragile source class. The dose series cannot say anything about fading on TaCT until the threshold question is settled.
3. **The theory note's B postdiction is split.** It predicted detection falling before ASR for global triggers, which holds on CIFAR-10 Blend and BPP and fails on CIFAR-100 Blend, GTSRB LF and Tiny WaNet. The attractor tail explains part of the split through the threshold, and the aggregation form must explain the rest. Experiment O4 (directional factor on faded hits) is the planned test.
4. **3 passes and 1 seed.** Every dose record uses 3 passes and seed 0, and `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1` has seeds 1 and 2 on disk. The collapse regime rests on 1 Blend model and 1 BPP model.
5. **Hit TPRs are reconstructed.** The table's hit TPRs assume misses are flagged at the clean rate. A per-sample dump of score, predicted class and target flag would make them exact and is needed for every idea below.
6. **The caller's example mixes regimes.** "ASR 0.93 with TPR 0.16" is CIFAR-10 Blend at dose 0.6, at 10% FPR, where the hit AUROC is 0.517. The "0.94" hit AUROC is GTSRB LF or CIFAR-10 BadNets at dose 0.5. The window is real in both, with a different cause in each.
7. **The window at 1% FPR is mostly the attractor class on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB.** Between 4.5% and 8.4% of clean validation images never flip, all from 1 non-target class per model. Any fix evaluated at 1% FPR must say whether it changed the treatment of those images.

## Ranked ideas, inference time

Each idea says what it would change, what the theory predicts and what would confirm or refute it. GPU costs are for the 8 backdoored ViT models of experiment B plus the 2 benign probes, reusing `experiments/evidence_surplus/trigger_dose/measure.py` (10 seconds per model at 3 passes). The development protocol applies: design on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB and read CIFAR-100 and Tiny once.

**a1. Class-conditional and 2-sided thresholds on a per-sample dump (CPU after 2 GPU-minutes).** Dump per-sample PSU, predicted class and hit flag at every dose, then threshold each input against the clean validation images predicted as its class, with the finite-sample conformal quantile, 1-sided and 2-sided. This changes the decision rule the canon fixes (`defenses/decision.py`), so it is a test first. The theory predicts that on the 5 CIFAR-10 and GTSRB models the hit TPR at 1% FPR rises to about the pooled rule's TPR at 5% to 10% FPR, for example from 0.30 to at least 0.45 on CIFAR-10 Blend at dose 0.8, with every class's realized FPR within $q + 1/(n_c+1)$, and that CIFAR-100 and Tiny change by at most 0.02. The 2-sided variant should catch the surplus-poor hits (CIFAR-10 Blend at 0.5 with hit AUROC 0.405 and CIFAR-10 WaNet at 0.33 to 0.43) at half the per-tail budget and gain nothing at Blend 0.6. A mean gain below 0.05 on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB at 1% FPR refutes the attractor account of the 1% window. The same dump gives the target-class null for contradiction 1.

**a2. A matched filter along the backdoor direction, bootstrapped from confident catches (about 1 GPU-minute per model).** Estimate $u$ at the last block's class token from the full-dose triggered inputs PSBD-TM flags at 1% FPR without ground-truth labels. Then score every faded input by $\langle h(x), u\rangle$. This adds a second statistic that reads firing and not surplus. The theory predicts a hit AUROC of at least 0.9 at every dose with ASR at least 0.5 on all 8 models, including CIFAR-10 Blend at 0.6 and 0.5, because firing requires crossing the head's boundary along $u$. CIFAR-10 WaNet flags too few inputs at full dose (TPR 0.016) to bootstrap, so there $u$ comes from the top singular direction of the target-class query pool, as in spectral signatures. A hit AUROC below 0.75 on CIFAR-10 Blend at dose 0.6 refutes it. It is the strongest candidate because it is the only one that escapes the surplus bound by construction, and its cost is a threat model with some confident catches.

**a3. Pooled class-conditional monitoring (CPU on the a1 dump).** Pool the scores of queries predicted as each class over a window and test them against that class's clean reference with a 1-sided rank test, then hand the alarm to a2. The prediction is the power table: 3 to 9 queries when the attack dominates the target's traffic in the gate regime and in BPP's partial collapse, and no detection at CIFAR-10 Blend dose 0.6. A detection there with fewer than 1000 queries would contradict the surplus bound. The ported detectors can be pooled the same way.

**a4. Cross-axis smoothing for global triggers (about 2 GPU-minutes per model).** Replace the prediction by the majority over 16 passes of Gaussian input noise at the $\sigma$ that costs at most 2 points of clean accuracy, and read ASR per dose. The inference in the purification section predicts that on CIFAR-10 Blend, BPP and GTSRB LF the smoothed ASR falls below 0.5 at a dose above $a_{\text{det}}$, so every dose is either detected by PSBD-TM or purified by smoothing. A smoothed ASR above 0.5 at the dose where the hit AUROC first drops below 0.6 refutes it. Same-axis purification by masked majority is the control, and the theory predicts it removes almost nothing at Blend 0.6.

**a5. Multi-rate $p^*$ scoring with 10 and 30 passes (about 2 GPU-minutes per model).** Score every faded input by its ladder $p^*$ and by PSU at 10 and 30 passes. The theory predicts a gain of at most 0.05 hit AUROC at doses where it is between 0.7 and 0.9 and no gain at CIFAR-10 Blend dose 0.6. It is ranked last because the theory predicts it fails where it matters, and it is worth running because a gain at 0.6 would refute the claim that $p^*$ overlap sets the limit.

The same dose harness should run the ported detectors (STRIP, SCALE-UP, IBD-PSC calibrated, TeCo, TED, Beatrix) on 4 models, CIFAR-10 Blend, CIFAR-10 BadNets, GTSRB LF and CIFAR-10 WaNet, to test the split predicted in the detector table. CD-L's optimization is the slow one and can be left out. Patch certification (PatchCleanser style double masking) applies only to BadNets and TaCT. Those are already in the gate regime, so it is not ranked.

## Ranked ideas, training time

**b1. Partial-evidence training moves global triggers into the gate regime (10 seconds per model now, then 1 run of 3 hours).** First run the dose series on `vit_cifar10_adaptive_blend_0_1` and `vit_gtsrb_adaptive_blend_0_1`, whose triggers were trained on 50% of 16 cells, against `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1` and `vit_gtsrb_blend_0_1`. The theory predicts that the hit AUROC of Adaptive-Blend declines at most half as fast with the dose as plain Blend's, with a full-dose $p^*$ near the 0.53 of an 8-of-16 decision. If it holds, train ViT GTSRB Blend 10% with CutMix as a defender's augmentation and predict a hit TPR at 10% FPR of at least 0.8 at the dose where ASR is 0.9. A decline as fast as plain Blend's refutes the link between fragment training and aggregation form.

**b2. Mixed-intensity poisoning as the non-adaptive threat (1 run of 3 hours, plus the WaNet series now).** Train ViT GTSRB Blend 10% with poisoned images split between blend weights at 1.0 and 0.1 of the standard alpha, TITIM's mixing. The theory predicts $a_{\text{fire}}$ below 0.2, PSBD-TM at full dose at or above the standard cell, and a ratio $a_{\text{det}}/a_{\text{fire}}$ still bounded by $S_q$, so the evasion band shifts to near-invisible doses without widening in ratio. The WaNet strength series `vit_*_wanet_0_01_trig_s{1,2,4}` gives TITIM's overfitting direction for free: higher training strength should narrow the dose range that fires. A ratio above $S_q$ refutes the additive form for mixed training. This is also a candidate answer to the mentor's request for a non-adaptive attack.

**b3. SAM shifts the window without narrowing it (10 seconds per model).** Run the dose series on `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1_sam_rho_{0_05,0_1,0_15,0_2}` and on seeds 1 and 2 of `vit_cifar10_blend_0_1`. The theory predicts that $a_{\text{fire}}$ and $a_{\text{det}}$ fall together with rho and their ratio stays within the seed spread. A ratio that shrinks with rho beyond the seed spread would mean SAM changes the aggregation form and would revive SAM for the paper, which currently declares it out of scope.

**b4. Label smoothing on a global trigger (1 run of 3 hours, beside R5 and R8).** Add the dose readout to R5 and R8 at no cost and add a GTSRB Blend 10% run with label smoothing 0.1. The theory predicts that BadNets keeps its gate under label smoothing while Blend's window moves toward full dose, so Blend's hit AUROC at dose 0.8 falls by at least 0.1 against `vit_gtsrb_blend_0_1`. If Blend is unaffected, its surplus is not magnitude and the collapse regime needs another explanation.

**b5. Isolation or decoupling (implementation plus 3 hours per run).** Anti-Backdoor Learning or decoupled training on ViT GTSRB Blend 10%. The prediction is that whatever backdoor survives is weak and low-surplus, so evasive success falls because ASR falls, and PSBD-TM's AUROC on the survivors is lower than on the standard cell. It is ranked last because it answers a pipeline question the paper does not ask and needs new code.

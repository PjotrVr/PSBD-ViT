# Why prediction consistency separates backdoored inputs

**Superseded on 2026-09-29 for panel numbers.** This memo was written on the 57-model ViT panel of 2026-09-24. The panel is now the 54 models successful at the 2-point clean-accuracy bar (`paper/headline.tex`, `\HeadlinePairedCells`), which drops the only ViT SIG model and 2 WaNet models. Its panel means are kept as measured and are recomputed on the new panel once the analysis experiments of 2026-09-30 land.

PSBD flags an input when its predicted-class probability barely falls under k perturbed forward passes. The question this memo answers is why that works at all, in general and on ViT-B/16 and Swin-S in particular, and which published account of it survives contact with the models in this repository. Li et al. show the phenomenon on ResNet-18 and VGG only and explain it by a "neuron bias" effect. This project already has a causal account for patch triggers on ViT (residual routing plus a late class-token read) and several refutations. I place every explanation I could find in the literature next to that evidence, and for each one I give the claim, the evidence and architectures behind it, the reasons it would or would not carry over to ViT and Swin and 1 inference-only test with the number it predicts.

A status word marks every explanation. **Confirmed** means a measurement on our models agrees with the explanation's own prediction. **Refuted** means a measurement contradicts it. **Partial** means it holds for some attack categories or in a weaker form than stated. **Open** means no measurement yet. I number the literature explanations L1 to L27 and the folk explanations P1 to P12 so the ranking table can refer to them.

The statistic every prediction refers to is the fractional PSU of `defenses.scores`, the headline statistic of the paper. It divides the unperturbed confidence out of the paper's absolute PSU.

$$\phi(x) = 1 - \frac{\tfrac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} P_c\big(x;\,\xi_j\big)}{P_c(x)}, \qquad c = \arg\max_i P_i(x)$$

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| $x$ | the input image |
| $P_i(x)$ | softmax probability of class $i$ with no perturbation |
| $c$ | the class the unperturbed model predicts |
| $\xi_j$ | the random draw of the operator on pass $j$ (a dropout mask, a token mask, a noise sample) |
| $P_c(x;\xi_j)$ | probability of class $c$ on perturbed pass $j$ |
| $k$ | number of perturbed passes, 3 in the headline |
| $\phi(x)$ | fractional prediction shift uncertainty, low means flagged as poisoned |
| $p$ | the operator's rate, for token masking the probability that a token is masked in 1 block on 1 pass |
| $m$ | number of patch tokens a trigger changes, 4 or 1 on our datasets |
| $H$ | Hessian of $P_c$ in the activation at the probe site (used under P4 and L2) |
| $\Sigma$ | covariance of the operator's perturbation at the probe site (used under P4 and L2) |

Two probabilities recur below. $p^m$ is the chance that every token of an $m$-token trigger is masked in a single masking event, and $p^{4m}$ is the chance that this happens in each of the 4 late blocks that read the trigger, which section B of `experiments/why_token_masking_works/` identified as the event that breaks a triggered BadNets prediction under PSBD-TM.

## Plausible explanations that may not hold

These are the explanations a reader, a reviewer or the PSBD authors would reach for first. Some are stated in print and some are folklore. For each I name who says it, why it sounds right and the measurement on our models that shows it wrong or would show it wrong. A refuted explanation is kept here because it is the answer to a reviewer's first question.

**P1. The backdoored model is just more confident on triggered inputs.** This is the skeptical reading raised by this project's own adversarial verification pass (`experiments/psu_vs_confidence/README.md`), and it is implicit in every margin account (Rajabi et al., MDTD) and in the observation that a saturated softmax has flat second derivatives. It sounds right because triggered inputs reach ASR near 1 with softmax near 1, and absolute PSU is bounded above by the starting confidence, so a very confident input has more room to fall and a flat softmax moves little. The measurement that shows it wrong is already on disk. The confidence-only detector reads 0.684 over the 57 models against PSBD-TM's 0.953, and the confidence-null record reads 0.658 against 0.887 with PSU ahead on 20 of 28 checkpoints (`\ConfidenceNullAuroc`, `\ConfidenceNullPsuAuroc`). The fractional statistic divides the starting confidence out and scores as well or better, and at PSBD-TM baseline confidence explains 0.020 of the statistic's variance (Q35 in `docs/open-questions.md`). Confidence carries part of the BadNets signal (0.742) and almost none of WaNet's (0.579). Status: refuted as the carrier.

**P2. Dropout at inference is a Bayesian ensemble, so PSBD is epistemic uncertainty estimation.** Li et al. frame PSBD as "model predictive uncertainty" and run MC-Dropout in their pilot study, following Gal and Ghahramani. It sounds right because the trigger pattern appears in many training images, so the posterior should be sharp on it, and a sharp posterior means low variance across dropout samples. 3 facts argue against it on our models. Gal and Ghahramani's reading requires the network to be trained with the same dropout so that sampling masks approximates a posterior, and every model here trains with dropout 0.0 (`models/backbones.py`) and is probed at rates from 0.05 to 0.8, which approximates no posterior. Ledda et al. show that dropout injected after training needs a separate rescaling to be a usable uncertainty at all. Deterministic perturbations detect as well as sampled ones: the calibrated IBD-PSC port uses 1 deterministic pass per amplification factor and reads 0.921 on the same 57 models. And the site matters more than the sampling, since the same Gaussian noise reads 0.183 lower than token masking at the attention input because a LayerNorm absorbs it (L25), which a posterior account cannot express. The decisive measurement is a real epistemic estimate: the disagreement of the 3 seed replicates on 14 cells (`\SeedCells`). If P2 holds, ensemble disagreement separates triggered from clean at an AUROC near PSBD-TM's, at least 0.9. I expect it to read far lower, near 0.6, because the 3 seeds agree on most clean test images and on nearly all triggered ones. Status: open, and argued against.

**P3. The trigger neurons are simply more robust.** Li et al. write that PSBD works by "utilizing robust neuron bias paths", and the Fine-pruning and ANP line treats backdoors as a small set of units that either sit dormant on clean data or respond to perturbation differently. It sounds right because the trigger-to-target association is the strongest the model learned. On our ViTs there is no such unit set in the residual stream: zeroing the top 300 of 768 coordinates leaves ASR at 1.00 while removing 1 rank-1 direction takes it to 0.00 (H16). The trigger path is not robust in itself either. Dropout on the 4 trigger positions alone keeps 0.387 of triggered BadNets predictions against 0.398 for dropout everywhere, and masking the trigger's tokens in all 12 blocks keeps 0.002 (`experiments/why_token_masking_works/`, sections A and C). Robustness also fails to transfer across modalities, since Fourier amplitude perturbation keeps ASR at 0.627 against clean 0.718 on GTSRB (H46). The survival PSBD-TM measures comes from where the operator acts relative to the trigger's route. Status: refuted for the residual basis. The MLP hidden basis is untested (L7).

**P4. Flat minima around the trigger.** This project's own curvature framing (`docs/theory-perturbation-consistency.md`) and the published derivations of DetectGPT and NETE say perturbation consistency estimates a Hessian trace, and SAM-based detection (Zhang et al.) assumes flatter backdoor features are easier to find. It sounds right because the second-order expansion gives $\phi \approx -\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(H\Sigma)/P_c$ for a zero-mean operator of covariance $\Sigma$, and a triggered input far from the boundary sits in a flat region. 3 observations weaken it. With isotropic $\Sigma$ the account predicts that isotropic noise matches or beats structured masks, and at the attention input Gaussian noise reads 0.839 against token masking's 0.953 at the adaptive rule and 0.183 below it at the matched rule. The structure of $\Sigma$ (whole tokens) carries the gain, and "flatness along the directions the trigger does not use" restates the routing account. Karim et al. report that backdoored models sit in sharper parameter-space minima than benign ones, so the sign depends on the space in which flatness is read. And Dinh et al. show that curvature changes under reparameterization that leaves the function unchanged. The measurement that decides it is a Hutchinson trace with the `rademacher` operator at small scale at `before_attention_norm`. The flatness account predicts that the trace reads at least PSBD-TM's AUROC. The routing account predicts it reads with the isotropic operators, about 0.84. Status: open, partly contradicted.

**P5. The triggered input is out of distribution.** Doan et al. attribute ViT's response to patch processing to how the trigger sits among the patches, and the general out-of-distribution literature treats unusual inputs as unstable. It sounds right because a BadNets square or a blended cartoon is not a natural image. The benign references refute it directly. The same triggered images are just as unusual for a benign model, and on the benign references PSU reads 0.505 (`\ConfidenceNullBenignPsu`) with a shift-to-target share of 0.002. A trigger that were detected for being unusual would be detected on a benign model too. The out-of-distribution intuition also has the wrong sign, since unusual inputs usually lose confidence faster under perturbation, which would push them to high PSU. Status: refuted.

**P6. Clean predictions collapse onto the target under perturbation.** This is Li et al.'s neuron bias account, and IBD-PSC's Theorem 3.1, BAN and Niu et al. make the same claim for amplified features, adversarial neuron noise and adversarial inputs. It sounds right because the backdoor installs the strongest class association in the network, so when clean evidence is removed the model falls back on it. The shifted clean predictions of our models land on the target at a mean share of 0.197 at the matched rule, and the share is 0.008 on Blend under PSBD-TM, where AUROC is 0.978. Blend, SIG and TaCT never exceed an excess of 0.02 on any dataset (`\ShiftToTargetNoDriftAttacks`). Attraction exists for WaNet (0.428 under PSBD-TM, 0.604 under PSBD-RD, up to +0.996 excess on GTSRB) and in part for BadNets, LF and BPP. It correlates with PSBD-RD's advantage over PSBD-TM (Spearman 0.39) and barely with PSBD-TM's own AUROC (0.22). Status: refuted as the carrier, present as a modifier (L10).

**P7. The trigger is small, so random masking rarely hits it.** This is the natural reading of token masking on patch triggers and the premise of this project's H27. It sounds right because a 4-token trigger is fully masked at rate $p$ with probability $p^4$, which is 0.06 at $p=0.5$. It is incomplete in 2 ways. Global triggers occupy every token and survive token masking as well as patches do (Blend 0.978, LF 0.980). And the same masking applied once at the embedding does hit the patch trigger: triggered BadNets predictions shift at 0.583 there, far above $p^m$ for the 4-token models, and AUROC falls to 0.787. What protects the trigger under PSBD-TM is that a masked token keeps its residual entry, so the prediction breaks only when every trigger token is masked in each of the roughly 4 late blocks that read it, which has probability $p^{4m}$ (section B of `why_token_masking_works`). Status: partial, replaced by L20.

**P8. Backdoors are learned earlier and so are embedded more deeply.** Anti-Backdoor Learning and the shortcut literature (Geirhos et al.) report that poisoned samples reach low loss in the first epochs. It sounds right because an early, easy feature should also be a stable one. Speed of learning predicts nothing about which injection site separates, and the whole placement result is about sites. The test is the queued `early_loss_signal` smoke, where the account predicts a first-drop AUROC of at least 0.9 on BPP and TaCT at 5% and an attack ordering that matches PSBD-TM's. Status: open (L4).

**P9. The triggered representation is collapsed, so perturbations cannot move it.** This project's rank-collapse finding and the latent separability literature (Tran et al., Qi et al.) suggest a low-dimensional triggered cluster that noise cannot scatter. It sounds right because a point deep inside a tight cluster has a large margin. The collapse measures correlate with detection only weakly once ASR is held fixed (partial Spearman -0.310 for the rank ratio, -0.342 for CKA and -0.157 inside the high-ASR band), and the classifier head's 668-dimensional null space lets the rank ratio move from 0.210 to 0.999 with logits unchanged to 1.9e-5 (`experiments/latent_geometry_predicts_detection/`). Nothing forces the collapse, so it cannot be the mechanism. Status: refuted as the mechanism, a weak correlate.

**P10. The target class is simply easy.** Nobody states this in print as a PSBD mechanism, and a reviewer would. If the target class were intrinsically robust, any image predicted as the target would score low, triggered or not, and PSBD would be detecting predictions of the target class. It sounds right because a poisoned target class receives more training images with a strong feature attached. The measurement is on disk: fractional PSU of clean validation images whose true class is the target, against clean images of other classes, on the backdoored model and on the benign reference of the same dataset. If P10 holds, at least half of clean target-class images fall below the 0.25 validation quantile on the backdoored model and not on the benign one. I expect about 0.25, the rate of any class. Status: open, CPU only.

**P11. Trigger features have a larger norm.** IBD-PSC's Theorem 3.1 rests on feature norm and Zhu et al. (FT-SAM) find larger weight norms on backdoor-related neurons. Darcet et al. show that ViTs repurpose high-norm tokens. It sounds right because a large-norm feature dominates a dot product. On our models the BadNets trigger tokens do carry a raised norm (peak max-over-median patch-norm ratio 4.26 against 3.65 for the benign model, `\RoutingBadnetRatioPeak`), but global triggers have no special token to be large, and amplifying every normalized feature by up to 15 times sends at most 0.223 of shifted clean predictions to the target where the theorem predicts nearly all of them. Status: partial for patch triggers, refuted as a general mechanism (L14).

**P12. Attention is hijacked by the trigger.** BadViT (Yuan et al.) builds an attack that pulls attention onto the trigger patch, and Subramanya et al. find that attention-based interpretation maps light up the trigger. It sounds right because ViT reads through attention. The static signature does not detect: per-sample attention entropy in the backdoor heads reads 0.48 to 0.54 AUROC for most attacks (H40). The dynamic role is real, since the class token puts 0.550 of its attention mass on the 4 BadNets trigger tokens in blocks 9 to 12 against 0.025 on a benign model (`\RoutingBadnetClsLate`). Status: refuted as a signature, confirmed as the route (L20, L21).

## Margin, smoothing and curvature

**L1. Perturbation consistency measures the distance to the decision boundary.** *Papers.* Fawzi et al. (NeurIPS 2016) bound robustness to random noise by the distance to the boundary with curvature as a correction. Cohen et al. (ICML 2019) certify a radius for a Gaussian-smoothed classifier from the top-class probability under noise. Rajabi et al. (MDTD, ACM CCS 2023) state that "input samples containing a Trojan trigger are located relatively farther away from a decision boundary than clean samples" and detect them by an estimated certified radius. Liu et al. (TrojDef, IEEE TNNLS) perturb inputs with Gaussian noise and write that Trojan training "makes Trojan inputs more stable than benign ones". Peng et al. (NETE, Neural Networks 2025) and Mitchell et al. (DetectGPT, ICML 2023) derive the same consistency statistic by a second-order expansion for text. *Claim.* The expected drop of the predicted-class probability under a small zero-mean perturbation is, to leading order, a decreasing function of the margin. A triggered input sits far from every boundary in the direction the trigger pushes, so it barely moves. Clean inputs sit closer to some boundary and move more.

*Evidence and architectures.* Fawzi et al. prove their bounds for general classifiers and check them on ConvNet image classifiers. Cohen et al. evaluate ResNet-50 on ImageNet and ResNet-110 on CIFAR-10. MDTD reports certified radii on CIFAR-100 of 0.005 for clean against 0.657 for BadNets inputs, on WideResNets for CIFAR and smaller ConvNets for GTSRB and SVHN. TrojDef uses VGG and ResNet-50. None evaluates a transformer image classifier. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* The statement is architecture-free, so it carries over formally. The question is which margin. MDTD and TrojDef measure margin in input space, while PSBD perturbs an internal site and the relevant margin is along the directions the operator moves at that site. On ViT the output margin is the wrong one: the fractional statistic divides the output confidence out, confidence explains 0.020 of its variance, and a probe placed at the head equals 1 minus the confidence baseline to a mean absolute difference of 0.0019 over 83 checkpoints (`experiments/theory_predictions/`, P1). On Swin, whose mean-pooled readout draws on 30% to 47% of the last stage's tokens against ViT's 0.9% to 30% (`experiments/swin_mechanism/`), an internal token-mask margin should be closer to an output margin, because every token feeds the readout directly.

*Prediction on our models.* If one shared input-space margin drove all perturbation detectors, an MDTD-style radius would rank the 7 attack categories the way PSBD-TM does, a Kendall tau of at least 0.7 over the per-attack means. The routing account predicts a tau of 0.4 or less, with Blend and LF ranked high by both and WaNet and SIG swapped, because input-space noise and token masking read different geometry. The measurement is Gaussian pixel noise at `input_pixels` with the certified radius $\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})$ as the score, on the 57 models. *Status.* Refuted in its output-margin form, open in its input-space form.

**L2. Perturbation consistency measures local curvature.** *Papers.* Garg et al. (ICML 2024) show that the input-space curvature of the loss is high for memorized, mislabeled and duplicated samples. Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. (CURE, CVPR 2019) tie robustness to small input-space curvature. Camuto et al. (NeurIPS 2020) give the layer-indexed form $\mathbb{E}[\Delta L] = \mathbb{E}[\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_k \sigma_k^2 \operatorname{tr}(J_k^\top H_L J_k)]$ for Gaussian noise injected at several layers. Park and Kim (ICLR 2022) show that multi-head self-attention flattens the loss landscape, reducing Hessian eigenvalue magnitudes, while leaving negative eigenvalues. This project's `docs/theory-perturbation-consistency.md` casts PSBD, STRIP, SCALE-UP and IBD-PSC as estimators of $\operatorname{tr}(H\Sigma)$ at different sites. *Claim.* For a zero-mean perturbation of covariance $\Sigma$ at a site, the expected drop of $P_c$ is $-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(H\Sigma)$ to second order, with $H$ the Hessian of $P_c$ in the activation at that site. Triggered inputs live where that Hessian is small.

*Evidence and architectures.* Garg et al. use ResNets on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. CURE uses ResNets on CIFAR-10. Park and Kim compare ViT and ResNet on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. Camuto et al. use MLPs and small ConvNets. None measures backdoor inputs. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* The expansion holds for any smooth network, and ViT's GELU and softmax are smooth. Two ViT facts limit what it explains. LayerNorm removes 0.179 of an isotropic disturbance at the attention input and 0.128 at the MLP input and passes a token mask almost whole (`\AbsorptionAttentionInputGaussianRemoved`), so the effective $\Sigma$ after the norm is operator-dependent and site-dependent. And the operator that wins, token masking, has a $\Sigma$ that is block-diagonal over tokens, so $\operatorname{tr}(H\Sigma)$ reads the Hessian's mass on whole tokens. For a patch trigger that mass is set by how many late blocks read the trigger's tokens, which is the routing account in second-order notation. On Swin, windowed attention confines a token's Hessian coupling to its window until the shifted windows and patch merging spread it, so the token-block structure of $H$ should be sharper in early stages.

*Prediction on our models.* The trace reading predicts that Rademacher noise, which has the same covariance as Gaussian and lower estimator variance, matches Gaussian within 0.01 AUROC at each site at matched shift, and that its AUROC at the smallest rate on the ladder equals the AUROC at the next rate, since the trace is scale-free to leading order. It also predicts that at `before_mlp`, where no norm follows, isotropic noise beats token masking, which is already measured: +0.051 [+0.015, +0.087] (`\GaussianMinusTokenMaskMlp`). The measurement is a `rademacher` sweep at `before_mlp` and `before_attention_norm` through `cli.sweep`. *Status.* Partial. The site-dependence of the operator ranking is explained by LayerNorm (L25), and the trace reading has not yet been tested directly.

**L3. Sharpness-aware training makes backdoor samples easier to detect.** *Papers.* Zhang et al. (arXiv 2024) retrain the suspect model with SAM and report that poisoned-sample detectors, PSBD among them in spirit, separate better afterwards. Zhu et al. (FT-SAM, ICCV 2023) find that backdoor-related neurons have larger weight norms and use SAM fine-tuning to shrink them. Karim et al. (FIP, ACM CCS 2024) report that backdoored models converge to sharper parameter-space minima than benign ones, with larger Hessian trace and top eigenvalue. *Claim.* SAM strengthens the backdoor feature relative to clean features, so any detector that reads the backdoor feature's dominance gains.

*Evidence and architectures.* All three report their main results on ConvNets such as ResNet-18, on CIFAR-10, GTSRB and Tiny-ImageNet scale data. None isolates a transformer effect. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* SAM changes which minimum the optimizer finds, and ViTs trained with SAM are known to reach flatter minima, so the mechanism transfers in principle. Whether it helps detection depends on which site reads the feature. H6 found a gain for PSBD-TM at 10% poisoning and a loss for the published placement at every rate, which says SAM changes the residual stream geometry in a site-dependent way. This project's SAM sub-claim in H16 turned out to be a LayerNorm artifact.

*Prediction on our models.* If SAM makes the trigger's route more dominant, the class token's attention mass on BadNets trigger tokens in blocks 9 to 12 rises from 0.550 on the Adam checkpoints to above 0.65 on the matched `_sam_rho_0_1` checkpoints, and the $p^{4m}$ breakage law of section B keeps its exponent. The measurement reuses the routing record of `experiments/residual_stream_mechanism/cls_routing.py` on the SAM checkpoints. *Status.* Partial (H6), mechanism open.

## Shortcut features and minimal evidence

**L4. Backdoors are shortcuts that the network learns first.** *Papers.* Geirhos et al. (Nature Machine Intelligence 2020) define shortcut learning as the use of simple decision rules that work on the training distribution. Shah et al. (NeurIPS 2020) show that networks rely exclusively on the simplest predictive feature and ignore more complex ones. Li et al. (Anti-Backdoor Learning, NeurIPS 2021) observe that poisoned samples reach low loss faster than clean ones and that backdoor learning is tied to the target class. Khaddaj et al. (ICML 2023) argue that a trigger is a feature indistinguishable in kind from natural features and detect poisoned samples by their unusually strong effect in datamodels. Liu et al. (TeCo) speculate that "the model learns shortcuts for the backdoor trigger guided by the backdoor loss function". *Claim.* The trigger is the simplest feature that predicts the target perfectly, it is learned in the first epochs and it suppresses reliance on other features for triggered inputs. A prediction that rests on 1 simple feature survives removal of the rest of the input.

*Evidence and architectures.* ABL uses WRN-16-1 on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB and ResNet-34 on an ImageNet subset. Khaddaj et al. use ResNet-9 on CIFAR-10. Shah et al. use MLPs and ConvNets on synthetic and MNIST-CIFAR data. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Simplicity bias is a property of gradient descent on overparameterized models and holds for transformers. Pretrained ViTs fine-tuned for 15 epochs start with rich features, so the trigger competes with an already useful representation, which should make it faster to fit relative to clean fine-tuning. What the shortcut account does not supply is any reason for site dependence. A shortcut learned early should be equally robust to dropout after the residual add and to token masking before the attention norm, and it is not (0.712 against 0.992 on BadNets).

*Prediction on our models.* Per-sample training loss separates poisoned from clean training samples with a first-drop AUROC of at least 0.9 on BPP and TaCT at 5%, and the ranking of attacks by that AUROC matches the ranking by PSBD-TM's AUROC with a Kendall tau of at least 0.6. The measurement is `experiments/early_loss_signal/`, whose smoke job is written and not yet submitted. *Status.* Open.

**L5. Scaled prediction consistency and its optimized mask.** *Papers.* Pal et al. (MSPC, ICLR 2024) show that SCALE-UP's pixel scaling fails for some attacks and learn a mask that selects the pixels whose scaling best separates poisoned from clean samples. *Claim.* The consistency of backdoor samples under scaling is real but concentrated in a subset of the input, so an optimized mask recovers it where uniform scaling does not.

*Evidence and architectures.* ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet scale data. No transformer is evaluated. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Uniform pixel scaling is filtered by ViT's patch embedding, a linear map that commutes with scaling, and then by LayerNorm, which removes a uniform per-token scale entirely. A pixel scale therefore reaches the attention blocks only through the position embedding's relative weight and the clamp to the pixel range. That predicts SCALE-UP is weak on ViT exactly for triggers whose information survives normalization regardless of scale, and the measured SCALE-UP port reads 0.399 on BPP and 0.615 on WaNet against 0.968 on BadNets.

*Prediction on our models.* A token-level MSPC, which learns 1 mask over the 196 tokens on the validation split, recovers BPP to at least 0.8. The measurement needs a mask optimization over cached forward passes of `input_pixels_scale_up` split by token, which is GPU inference. *Status.* Open.

**L6. Backdoor inputs need a small sufficient evidence set.** *Papers.* Huang et al. (Cognitive Distillation, ICLR 2023) learn, per input, the smallest input mask that keeps the model's output, and find that "the CPs of backdoor samples are all surprisingly and suspiciously small". Naseer et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and Doan et al. supply the ViT half, that ViTs classify from subsets of patches. *Claim.* A triggered input's prediction is fully explained by the trigger region, so the minimal sufficient mask has a small L1 norm, while a clean prediction needs a large part of the object.

*Evidence and architectures.* VGG-16, ResNet-18, PreActResNet-101, MobileNetV2, GoogLeNet and EfficientNet-b0 on CIFAR-10, GTSRB and a 200-class ImageNet subset, over 12 attacks including BadNets, Blend, SIG and WaNet. Test-time AUROC 0.949 on average. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* The token is ViT's natural unit of evidence, so a token-level sufficient set is cleaner than a pixel mask. For a patch trigger the sufficient set is the trigger's tokens routed to the class token in the late blocks. For a global trigger any subset of tokens carries some of the trigger, so the sufficient set is small only if each token carries enough of it. Our ported CD-L reads 0.943 on BadNets, 0.921 on BPP and 0.599 on LF, which already says the pixel-level version captures BPP's per-pixel quantization and misses LF's smooth pattern. On Swin, patch merging mixes 4 neighbors at each stage boundary, so a sufficient set is a set of windows rather than tokens.

*Prediction on our models.* Section D of `why_token_masking_works` measured visible-subset retention on 24 models: at 30% visible tokens Blend, BPP, LF and SIG keep 0.90 to 0.95 of their excess ASR while clean accuracy keeps 0.11 to 0.52. WaNet keeps 0.183 against 0.113. A per-sample smallest sufficient token fraction, found by nested deterministic masks of decreasing size, should then have a median of 0.02 or less for triggered BadNets (1 to 4 tokens of 196), 0.10 or less for Blend, LF and BPP and 0.30 or more for clean images, with WaNet within 0.05 of clean. The AUROC of that fraction per attack should match PSBD-TM's per-attack AUROC within 0.05. The measurement is a deterministic `TokenMask` variant at `before_attention_norm` in all blocks with a fixed kept set per pass. *Status.* Confirmed in aggregate for global triggers except WaNet, per-sample form open.

## Neurons, directions and latent geometry

**L7. Backdoor neurons.** *Papers.* Liu et al. (Fine-Pruning, RAID 2018) find neurons that are dormant on clean inputs and fire on triggered ones, and prune them. Wu and Wang (ANP, NeurIPS 2021) find that backdoored models are sensitive to adversarial perturbations of individual neurons and prune the sensitive ones. Zheng et al. (CLP, ECCV 2022) flag channels with a large upper bound on their Lipschitz constant. Li et al. (RNP, ICML 2023) expose backdoor neurons by unlearning and then recovering. Xu et al. (BAN, NeurIPS 2024) activate the backdoor by adversarial neuron noise and detect backdoored models by it. Li et al.'s PSBD calls its own account "neuron bias". *Claim.* A small set of units carries the trigger-to-target computation. They are separable from clean units by activation, sensitivity or Lipschitz bound, and removing them removes the backdoor.

*Evidence and architectures.* Fine-Pruning uses face recognition, speech and traffic-sign ConvNets. ANP, CLP and RNP use ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10. BAN uses ResNet-18, VGG-16 and DenseNet-121, with a 12-layer Swin on CIFAR-10 in its appendix, where BAN detects all 5 backdoored models. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* A unit basis is privileged only where an elementwise nonlinearity acts on it. In a ViT the residual stream has no elementwise nonlinearity, only LayerNorm, which is rotation-equivariant up to its per-coordinate gain, so a backdoor there has no reason to align with coordinates. The MLP hidden layer passes through GELU and is privileged, so neurons could exist there. H16 confirms the first half: 300 of 768 residual coordinates zeroed leaves ASR at 1.00 and 1 direction removed takes it to 0.00. BAN's Swin result shows neuron-level noise can still activate a backdoor on a windowed transformer, which is compatible with a direction, since any direction has nonzero projection on many units. The ported channel mask on MLP hidden units reads 0.838 against PSBD-TM's 0.953, below token masking on BadNets (0.775) and TaCT (0.522) and close on the global triggers.

*Prediction on our models.* If backdoor neurons live in the MLP hidden basis, zeroing the top 30 of 3072 GELU units per block in blocks 9 to 12, ranked by the triggered-minus-clean mean activation at the trigger positions (the score `experiments/residual_stream_mechanism/register_neurons.py` already computes), drives ASR to 0.1 or below. If the direction account holds, ASR stays above 0.9 and within 0.05 of 30 random units. The measurement is a forward pass with a zeroing hook on `mlp.3`'s input. *Status.* Refuted for the residual stream, open for the MLP hidden basis.

**L8. The backdoor is a single linear direction in the residual stream.** *Papers.* Karayalçin et al. (arXiv 2026) derive a backdoor direction in ViT-B/16 from paired clean and triggered activations, and show it is causal by steering and by orthogonalizing it out of the output projections. They find that stealthy triggers (WaNet, SSBA, BPP) move to the class token in earlier layers than static ones because "these triggers introduce minor perturbations that can be detected in each token separately". Arditi et al. (NeurIPS 2024) show refusal in chat models is mediated by 1 direction, which is the method Karayalçin et al. port. Park et al. (ICML 2024) formalize the linear representation hypothesis. *Claim.* A single direction per attack and layer carries the backdoor and is written into the class token at an attack-dependent depth. Removing it removes the backdoor.

*Evidence and architectures.* Karayalçin et al. use BackdoorBench ViT-B/16 models on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet at 1%, 5% and 10% poisoning, with BadNets, Blend, BPP, WaNet, SSBA, TrojanNN and InputAware. Arditi et al. use 13 open chat models. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* This is native to the residual architecture and is the account this project's H16, H29 and H30 confirm on its own models: rank-1 removal takes ASR from 1.00 to 0.00 on every checkpoint (up to 0.572 left on the hardest all-to-one attack, `\AblationDirectionMaxAsr`), each attack learns its own direction (cross-attack cosine 0.023 to 0.053) and the direction crystallizes at blocks 8 to 10. Swin has a residual stream within each stage and a linear patch merging between stages, so a direction exists per stage, and Swin crystallizes it past 60% of depth as well (`experiments/swin_mechanism/`, onset table). One claim does not carry over to our models. Karayalçin et al.'s per-token account predicts that WaNet stays legible from a random subset of tokens, and section D measures WaNet at 0.183 excess retention at 30% visible tokens against 0.113 for clean images, while BPP keeps 0.942. Our `adversarial_direction` experiment also failed to reproduce their mid-layer WaNet cosine (negative on our checkpoints) while reproducing BPP's.

*Prediction on our models.* If PSU reads how much of the direction survives the perturbation, then on triggered inputs the per-sample Spearman correlation between fractional PSU and the fractional drop of the block-12 class-token projection onto the direction, measured under the same masks, is at least 0.7 for every category. `experiments/dropout_kills_direction/` measured the aggregate form on 1 BadNets model (normalized separation 0.696 at rate 0.1 under pre-residual dropout, 0.015 under post-residual) and needs the per-sample form on the panel. For the WaNet conflict, the per-token projection onto the WaNet direction at blocks 4 to 8 should be spread evenly over tokens (coefficient of variation under 0.5) if Karayalçin et al.'s reading holds on our models, and concentrated otherwise. *Status.* Confirmed as the representation, open as the quantity PSU reads, contradicted for WaNet's per-token legibility.

**L9. Spectral signatures and latent separability.** *Papers.* Tran et al. (NeurIPS 2018) find that poisoned samples of a class leave a large projection on the top singular vector of the class's feature covariance. Chen et al. (activation clustering, 2018) cluster last-layer activations into 2 groups per class. Hayase et al. (SPECTRE, ICML 2021) whiten with robust statistics first. Qi et al. (ICLR 2023) show the separability is an assumption an attacker can break, with Adaptive-Blend and Adaptive-Patch. *Claim.* Triggered samples form a separable cluster in late features, and the separation is along few directions.

*Evidence and architectures.* ResNets and small ConvNets on CIFAR-10, MNIST and LISA. Qi et al. use ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Separability of late features carries over trivially, since a working backdoor must move triggered inputs to the target's region. It does not explain perturbation consistency, because separability says where the cluster is and not how stable it is. On our models separability predicts detection only through ASR (partial Spearman 0.095 once ASR is held fixed), and it reaches 0.96 on a benign model probed with a trigger it never learned (`experiments/latent_geometry_predicts_detection/`). Qi et al.'s asymmetric Adaptive-Blend is the relevant attack for us: it poisons with a random half of 16 trigger pieces at opacity 0.15 and tests with all pieces at 0.2, which trains the model to fire from partial triggers. That predicts robustness to token masking by construction, and H46 found Adaptive-Blend anti-fragile (ASR retention 1.28 and 1.19 under dropout). Most Adaptive-Blend models fall below the ASR bar on our panel, so it is not in the 57.

*Prediction on our models.* On the Adaptive-Blend models that do implant, triggered survival under PSBD-TM should exceed 0.95 at visible fractions down to 0.5, since training covered half the pieces. The measurement is section D's visible-subset protocol on those checkpoints. *Status.* Refuted as the mechanism of consistency, relevant as an attack design.

**L10. Perturbation drives clean inputs onto the target class.** *Papers.* Li et al. (PSBD, CVPR 2025) posit that "neurons become predisposed to features highly representative of certain classes". Under dropout, they argue, clean inputs lose their discriminative features and fall back on the bias while triggered inputs keep their prediction because the bias favors the target. Hou et al. (IBD-PSC, ICML 2024) prove in Theorem 3.1 that amplifying batch-norm parameters makes the feature norm large enough that the backdoored model predicts the target for benign inputs. Xu et al. (BAN) find adversarial neuron noise flips clean inputs to the target. Niu et al. (IEEE TPAMI 2024) observe that adversarial examples against backdoored models tend to land on the target class. *Claim.* The backdoor installs an attractor. Any perturbation strong enough to remove clean evidence pushes clean inputs toward the target, which lowers their predicted-class probability, while triggered inputs already sit at the attractor.

*Evidence and architectures.* PSBD shows 64 of the 512 final feature maps of 1 BadNets ResNet-18 with and without dropout and a histogram of shifted labels on a benign model, and its main experiments use ResNet-18 and VGG-16 on CIFAR-10, GTSRB and Tiny-ImageNet. IBD-PSC's theorem assumes features follow a Gaussian mixture and is checked on ResNet-18 and MobileNet on CIFAR-10, GTSRB and a 200-class ImageNet subset. Niu et al. use ConvNets. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* An attractor needs the backdoor direction to dominate the logits once clean evidence is gone. In ViT the backdoor directions of 9 of 10 attacks are nearly orthogonal to the target's readout weight (87 to 91 degrees, H36), and only BadNets aligns (33 to 43 degrees). That predicts attraction where the direction projects on the target logit and not elsewhere. LayerNorm also caps the feature norm that IBD-PSC's theorem needs, since amplifying a normalization output by a factor is followed by further normalizations in the next block. On Swin the same argument applies at every stage.

*Prediction on our models.* The attractor account predicts a target share of shifted clean predictions near 1 and a strong correlation between target share and AUROC. Measured, the share is 0.197 at the matched rule with no drift at all on Blend, SIG and TaCT, the Spearman correlation with PSBD-TM's AUROC is 0.22, and under IBD-PSC's own amplification at factor 15 the share is 0.001 to 0.223 while 0.89 to 0.97 of clean predictions shift. What remains is the modifier: attraction correlates with PSBD-RD's advantage over PSBD-TM (0.39, p 0.003), and it is largest on WaNet, the category where PSBD-RD leads (0.956 against 0.845). The decisive CPU test recomputes fractional PSU from the cached per-pass probabilities with the target column removed and the remaining probabilities renormalized. If attraction carries PSBD-RD's WaNet result, its WaNet AUROC falls from 0.956 to 0.85 or below. If clean fragility carries it and attraction only decides where the lost mass goes, AUROC moves by less than 0.02. *Status.* Refuted as the carrier on ViT, partial as a modifier, 1 CPU test away from decided.

## The perturbation-consistency detector family

**L11. STRIP, entropy under superposition.** *Papers.* Gao et al. (STRIP, ACSAC 2019) superimpose the test input with clean images and compute the entropy of the predictions. *Claim.* The trigger dominates any image it is blended into, so superposed triggered inputs keep predicting the target and their entropy is low. Clean inputs blended with other images scatter.

*Evidence and architectures.* A 2-conv network on MNIST and ResNet20 on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB, with square, heart and Hello Kitty triggers. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Superposition at weight 0.5 halves both images in pixel space. A patch trigger stays legible at half contrast in its tokens and is routed as before, so STRIP should transfer for patch triggers. A blended trigger whose training opacity was already low falls below the model's detection threshold when halved again, and a warp is averaged away by blending with an unwarped image. TeCo's authors report that STRIP's performance depends on the backbone. Our port reads 0.968 on BadNets, 0.922 on Blend, 0.519 on WaNet and 0.656 on TaCT, where TaCT's trigger fires only with source-class content that the superposition dilutes.

*Prediction on our models.* STRIP's entropy AUROC should fall monotonically with the blend weight given to the clean overlay for Blend and LF, and not for BadNets. At an overlay weight of 0.7 on the clean image, Blend should read at most 0.7 while BadNets stays above 0.9. The measurement is the ported `strip` detector with its weight exposed. *Status.* Measured as a detector, mechanism consistent.

**L12. MC dropout and dropout injection.** *Papers.* Gal and Ghahramani (ICML 2016) show dropout at test time approximates Bayesian inference in a deep Gaussian process when the network was trained with the same dropout. Ledda et al. (Information Sciences 2023) study dropout injected only at test time into a network trained without it and find it needs a separate rescaling of the uncertainty to be competitive. *Claim.* The spread of predictions under test-time dropout measures epistemic uncertainty, which is low on inputs the model has effectively memorized.

*Evidence and architectures.* MLPs and ConvNets for classification and regression, crowd-counting ConvNets for injection. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Our models train with dropout 0.0, so the Bayesian reading does not apply, and PSBD becomes dropout injection in Ledda et al.'s sense. The discussion is under P2.

*Prediction on our models.* See P2. A seed-ensemble disagreement AUROC of at least 0.9 if the uncertainty reading holds. *Status.* Open, argued against.

**L13. SCALE-UP, scaled prediction consistency.** *Papers.* Guo et al. (SCALE-UP, ICLR 2023) multiply pixel values by increasing factors and measure how often the prediction stays. *Claim.* Under a neural tangent kernel model with an RBF kernel, Theorem 1 shows that when the number of poisoned samples approaches the number of benign samples, or the model overfits poisoned samples, the scaled poisoned input $n \cdot x'$ is still predicted as the target for every $n \geq 1$.

*Evidence and architectures.* ResNet-34 and VGG-19 on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet against BadNets, label-consistent, PhysicalBA, TUAP, WaNet and ISSBA. The authors name ViT as future work. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* The theorem's regime, poisoned samples comparable in number to benign ones, is far from 1% to 10% poisoning, so the theorem predicts consistency that grows with the poison rate. The patch embedding and LayerNorm argument under L5 applies as well. On Swin, LayerNorm sits at the start of each block too, so the same filtering happens.

*Prediction on our models.* The theorem predicts SCALE-UP's AUROC rises with the poison rate for every attack. Measured with the project's `input_pixels_scale_up` placement, BadNets is flat (0.979, 0.982, 0.976 at 1%, 5%, 10%), Blend rises (0.780, 0.758, 0.886), LF is not monotone (0.684, 0.779, 0.667) and BPP falls (0.472, 0.460, 0.369). *Status.* Partial, holds for Blend and fails for BPP and LF.

**L14. IBD-PSC, parameter-oriented scaling consistency.** *Papers.* Hou et al. (IBD-PSC, ICML 2024) amplify the scale and shift of several batch-norm layers and measure the consistency of the prediction. *Claim.* Theorem 3.1: if the batch-normalized feature follows a Gaussian mixture, amplifying the batch-norm parameters makes its norm arbitrarily large, and past a constant $M$ the backdoored model predicts the target $t$ even for inputs it classified otherwise. Poisoned inputs, already at $t$, stay.

*Evidence and architectures.* ResNet-18, PreActResNet-18 and MobileNet on CIFAR-10, GTSRB and a 200-class ImageNet subset. No transformer is evaluated. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* ViT normalizes with LayerNorm where the ResNets use batch norm, so our port amplifies the LayerNorm outputs (`gain_scale`). The theorem's mechanism is the attractor of L10. The empirical phenomenon transfers: the calibrated port reads 0.921 on the 57 models and PSBD-TM leads it by only +0.032 [+0.007, +0.057]. The theorem's mechanism does not, since at factor 15 at most 0.223 of shifted clean predictions reach the target.

*Prediction on our models.* The theorem predicts a target share that rises toward 1 with the amplification factor. The measurement is already made above and reads 0.001 to 0.223 at the top of the ladder. A further cheap test is the target share as a function of the factor across the whole ladder, where the theorem predicts a monotone rise past some factor and the fragility account predicts a flat share. *Status.* Detector confirmed on ViT, theorem refuted as the mechanism.

**L15. TeCo, corruption robustness consistency.** *Papers.* Liu et al. (TeCo, CVPR 2023) corrupt the input with 15 corruption types at 5 severities and record, per type, the severity at which the prediction first changes. The spread of those severities across types is the score. *Claim.* Clean inputs have similar robustness to every corruption type. Triggered inputs are robust to some corruptions and fragile to others, because the trigger is a shortcut that some corruptions destroy (blur on a patch) and others leave (noise on a patch). The authors attribute this to the dual-target backdoor loss.

*Evidence and architectures.* PreActResNet-18, MobileViT-xs, WideResNet-101-2 and Swin-T-Base on CIFAR-10, GTSRB, Tiny-ImageNet and a 200-class ImageNet subset. On Swin-T-Base it averages 0.985 AUROC over BadNets, Blended, Input-aware, WaNet, LIRA and SSBA. It is the only member of this family evaluated on a Swin in its original paper. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* TeCo's signal is input-space inconsistency, which H46 also found: backdoors are robust to activation dropout and not to Fourier amplitude changes. Our ViT port reads 0.976 on BadNets and 0.901 on WaNet and fails on LF (0.477) and BPP (0.562), triggers that corruptions affect uniformly with the image. Its Swin result predicts it should do well on our Swin models.

*Prediction on our models.* On the Swin panel the ported `teco` detector should read at least 0.95 mean AUROC, its own Swin-B level, if the inconsistency is a property of windowed transformers. The measurement is `cli.baselines` on the Swin cells. *Status.* Measured on ViT, open on Swin.

**L16. BaDExpert and TED, consistency in parameter space and across depth.** *Papers.* Xie et al. (BaDExpert, ICLR 2024) fine-tune the suspect model on a few deliberately mislabeled clean samples, which erases clean behavior and keeps the backdoor, and flag inputs on which this "backdoor expert" agrees with the original model. Mo et al. (TED, IEEE S&P 2024) track, layer by layer, the rank of an input's nearest neighbors in the predicted class and flag inputs whose trajectory jumps to the target early. *Claim.* The backdoor function is more robust to parameter change than clean functions (BaDExpert), and triggered inputs commit to their class earlier in depth (TED).

*Evidence and architectures.* BaDExpert uses ResNet, VGG, MobileNetV2 and ViT. TED reports ConvNet image classifiers. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* BaDExpert's robustness to fine-tuning is the parameter-space analogue of PSU, and BaDExpert's own ViT result says it transfers. TED's early commitment conflicts with what we measure for patch triggers on ViT: the BadNets trigger lives in its own tokens until block 7 and reaches the class token after block 9 (H49), so the class token commits late. Our TED port still reads 0.909 on BadNets and 0.965 on TaCT, which suggests it reads the patch tokens' neighborhoods rather than the class token's. `experiments/prediction_depth/` found the same split: agreement across all tokens reads 0.982 on 1 BadNets model while class-token depth reads 0.826.

*Prediction on our models.* If TED's signal is early commitment of the class token, restricting its features to the class token drops it by at least 0.1 on BadNets. If it reads patch tokens, it stays within 0.02. The measurement is the ported `ted` detector with the token reduction switched. *Status.* Measured as detectors, mechanism open.

**L17. Consistency under perturbation in language models.** *Papers.* Yang et al. (RAP, EMNLP 2021) insert a rare word perturbation calibrated to drop clean confidence by a threshold and flag inputs whose confidence barely drops. Peng et al. (NETE) and Mitchell et al. (DetectGPT) use perturbation discrepancy. *Claim.* Poisoned inputs anchored by a trigger are robust to a perturbation that clean inputs are not.

*Evidence and architectures.* BERT-based classifiers and pretrained language models. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* This is the transformer precedent for PSBD's phenomenon, with tokens as the unit. RAP's perturbation is an added token, so the trigger tokens remain intact, which is the same reason PSBD-TM spares a patch trigger's residual entry.

*Prediction on our models.* None beyond L20. It places the result in the text literature. *Status.* Consistent.

## Token mechanics in Vision Transformers

**L18. Input patch processing breaks patch triggers.** *Papers.* Doan et al. (AAAI 2023) randomly drop or shuffle the image's patches before the position embedding and flag an input by how often its prediction changes. *Claim.* ViTs respond to patch transformations differently from ConvNets. Under PatchDrop, clean accuracy of a ViT falls very little even at about 50% dropped patches while the ASR of a patch trigger falls sharply, so a prediction that changes under PatchDrop is flagged. Under PatchShuffle, clean accuracy falls and the ASR of blend and warp triggers stays, so a prediction that survives shuffling is flagged. The authors summarize the patch case as "ASR sensitivity: ViT >> CNN".

*Evidence and architectures.* ViT and ConvNets on CIFAR-10, GTSRB and Tiny-ImageNet against BadNets, ReFool and WaNet. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* PatchDrop removes a token from the sequence for the whole network. For a patch trigger that removes the trigger's content, so the flag is on instability, the opposite sign from PSBD. Section A of `why_token_masking_works` measured the same thing in our models: masking the trigger tokens in all 12 blocks keeps 0.002 of triggered BadNets predictions. PSBD-TM spares the trigger because it masks only the attention input of each block independently, so the trigger token keeps its stream entry and 1 visible trigger token in 1 late block keeps most predictions. So Doan et al.'s sign and PSBD-TM's sign are both correct for their sites. On Swin, a dropped token would remove content from a window and from every later merged token, so PatchDrop should hurt Swin's patch triggers as well.

*Prediction on our models.* At the embedding, the cached `after_embedding_token_mask` should flip the sign if it behaves like PatchDrop. It does not flip it on average: BadNets reads 0.787 with triggered shift 0.583 against clean 0.884. The triggered shift on the 4-token models lies well above $p^m$, so losing part of the trigger breaks it, but not enough to fall below clean at the rates the adaptive rule picks (0.6 to 0.8). The next measurement records the masks at `after_embedding` as section B does and tabulates triggered survival by the number $j$ of surviving trigger tokens. The account predicts survival near 0 at $j = 0$ and a rise with $j$ that reaches at least 0.9 at $j = 4$, with $j = 1$ well below the 0.930 that a single late-block token achieves under PSBD-TM. *Status.* Confirmed as the reason PSBD's sign depends on the site. The exact survival curve at the embedding is open.

**L19. Occlusion robustness and token redundancy.** *Papers.* Naseer et al. (NeurIPS 2021) show that ViTs "retain as high as 60% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet even after randomly occluding 80% of the image content". Salman et al. (CVPR 2022) exploit that ViTs handle column ablations by dropping the ablated tokens, which makes derandomized smoothing both more accurate and faster. Rao et al. (DynamicViT, NeurIPS 2021), Liang et al. (EViT, ICLR 2022) and Bolya et al. (ToMe, ICLR 2023) drop or merge a large share of tokens in later layers with little accuracy loss, EViT keeping the tokens the class token attends to most. *Claim.* ViT classification evidence is spread over many tokens, so removing a random subset leaves the prediction.

*Evidence and architectures.* DeiT and ViT families on ImageNet, with ResNet-50 and BiT as ConvNet baselines. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Our models contradict the claim for clean inputs. At 30% visible tokens clean accuracy keeps only 0.11 to 0.52 of its level, and PSBD-TM's adaptive rate changes 0.87 of clean predictions. 3 differences explain it. Our images are 32 or 64 pixels upsampled to 224, so an object covers few original pixels and each token carries a small, highly correlated share. Our masking acts at the attention input of every block, which compounds. And our models are fine-tuned for 15 epochs on small datasets where the ViTs Naseer et al. test are trained on ImageNet at scale. Token redundancy does hold for global triggers, which is the half of the claim PSBD-TM needs. On Swin, mean pooling and patch merging make evidence redundant by construction.

*Prediction on our models.* If upsampled low-resolution inputs make clean predictions fragile, the clean shift ratio at a fixed token-mask rate of 0.3 on the benign references is highest on CIFAR (32 pixels) and lowest on Tiny (64 pixels), by at least 0.1. The measurement is the cached `before_attention_norm_token_mask` ladder on `vit_cifar10_benign`, `vit_cifar100_benign` and `vit_tiny_benign`, read at rate 0.3. *Status.* Confirmed for global triggers, contradicted for clean inputs on our models, the resolution explanation open and CPU only.

**L20. Residual routing and a late class-token read.** *Papers.* Raghu et al. (NeurIPS 2021) find that ViT skip connections are unusually influential and that there is a phase transition: "in the first half of the network, the CLS token representation is primarily propagated by the skip connection branch", while in the second half it takes most of its update from the attention and MLP branches. They also find that a class-token readout preserves each patch token's spatial location through the top layers, while global average pooling reduces it. Elhage et al. (2021) describe the residual stream as a communication channel into which each layer reads and writes. Geva et al. (EMNLP 2023) introduce attention knockout, blocking attention edges from specific tokens at specific layers to locate where information moves to the predicting position. *Claim.* Tokens keep their own content in the residual stream through depth, and the class token gathers from them mainly in the second half of the network. Information from a token can only reach the prediction by being read through attention at some layer.

*Evidence and architectures.* Raghu et al. use ViT-B, ViT-L and ViT-H trained on JFT and ImageNet against ResNets. Geva et al. use GPT-2 and GPT-J. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* This is the account this project measured causally for patch triggers. Masking the trigger tokens only in blocks 1 to 4 leaves 1.000 of triggered BadNets predictions, masking them in blocks 9 to 12 leaves 0.200 and in all 12 leaves 0.002, while random tokens leave 1.000 (section A). The class token's attention mass on the 4 trigger tokens is 0.025 in blocks 1 to 4, 0.231 in blocks 5 to 8 and 0.550 in blocks 9 to 12, against 0.025 on the benign model (`\RoutingBadnetClsEarly`, `\RoutingBadnetClsRouting`, `\RoutingBadnetClsLate`). Patching the 4 trigger tokens recovers the clean answer at 1.000 through block 6 (H49). Section A is Geva et al.'s knockout applied to backdoors. Swin has no class token and pools the mean of all final tokens, which by Raghu et al.'s finding reduces spatial localization at the top. Swin's readout draws on 30% to 47% of the last stage's tokens against ViT's attention-selected 0.9% to 30%. So on Swin a patch trigger's evidence must be spread into many tokens by windowed attention before the pool, and masking any of those tokens removes a share of it, which should make Swin's triggered predictions less protected by routing and more protected by redundancy.

*Prediction on our models.* On Swin, deterministic masking of the trigger's own tokens in every block (section A's protocol) should leave far more than ViT's 0.002, at least 0.5, because by the last stage the trigger's evidence lives in many merged tokens. The measurement ports section A to Swin with `trigger_tokens` mapped onto the Swin stage-1 grid. *Status.* Confirmed on ViT for patch triggers, open on Swin.

**L21. Attention localizes the trigger.** *Papers.* Subramanya et al. (arXiv 2022, WACV 2024) find that "interpretation maps have sharp highlights on the trigger patch" in ViTs and not in ConvNets (IoU 0.47 for ViT-Base against 0.039 for ResNet-50) and block the highest-scoring region at test time, cutting ASR on ViT-Base from 61.4% to 16.4% at a clean cost of 2.1 points. Yuan et al. (BadViT, CVPR 2023) design a patch-wise trigger that pulls attention away from the patches that support the true class. *Claim.* Attention concentrates on a patch trigger, so attention itself localizes the trigger and removing the attended region removes the backdoor.

*Evidence and architectures.* ViT-Base, PatchConv and CaiT on ImageNet with a 30 by 30 pixel trigger at 0.05% poisoning, against VGG-16, ResNet-18 and ResNet-50. BadViT uses DeiT and ViT. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* The routing measurement above confirms the concentration in the late blocks. It does not make attention a per-sample detector by itself: attention entropy in the backdoor heads reads 0.48 to 0.54 (H40), and 3 heads that diverge for every attack (H31) do not help as a PSBD operator (targeted 3-head masking 0.580, H35). On Swin, attention is local to windows, so a rollout map is a product of window-restricted maps and localizes a trigger to its window at best.

*Prediction on our models.* A detector that masks the top 4 tokens by class-token attention in blocks 9 to 12 and reads the prediction change should flag triggered BadNets with AUROC at least 0.95 in the inverted direction, since section A predicts triggered survival near 0.200 when those tokens are masked in the late blocks and clean survival above 0.96. The measurement uses the attention capture already in `cls_routing.py`. *Status.* Confirmed as localization, refuted as a static signature.

**L22. Missingness is native to transformers.** *Papers.* Jain et al. (ICLR 2022) show that blacking out pixels to simulate missing features biases ConvNet predictions toward certain classes, and that a transformer can implement missingness by dropping the corresponding tokens, which avoids that bias. *Claim.* Zeroed pixels are off the data manifold and shift predictions for reasons unrelated to the removed content. Dropping tokens removes content without that artifact.

*Evidence and architectures.* ResNet-50 and ViT on ImageNet. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* PSBD-TM zeroes the input to `ln_1`. LayerNorm maps a zero vector to its bias vector, so a masked token enters attention as the constant $\beta$ of that block's norm, the same for every masked position and every image. That is closer to Jain et al.'s missingness than to a blacked-out patch, since the masked token contributes an input-independent key and value. The `token_substitute` operator was built to test exactly this: it replaces a token with another token of the same image, which removes content and stays on the manifold. On Swin the same holds at `norm1`.

*Prediction on our models.* If the off-manifold component contributes nothing, `token_substitute` at `before_attention_norm` reads within 0.02 of PSBD-TM at matched shift. If PSBD-TM partly works because a constant token is off-manifold, substitution reads at least 0.05 lower. The measurement is a `cli.sweep` with `--operator token_substitute`. *Status.* Open.

**L23. Global triggers are legible from any token subset.** *Papers.* Karayalçin et al. argue that distributed triggers "can be detected in each token separately", which is the ViT form of Doan et al.'s observation that "ViTs learn and generalize the spatial invariance of the triggers extremely well" under PatchShuffle. *Claim.* A trigger present in every token lets any subset of tokens carry it, so random token removal leaves the triggered prediction while it degrades the clean one.

*Evidence and architectures.* ViT-B/16 (Karayalçin et al.) and ViT against ConvNets (Doan et al.). *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Section D confirms it for Blend, BPP, LF and SIG and refutes it for WaNet (numbers under L6). On Swin, windowed attention keeps a global trigger's evidence local until later stages, but every window holds a piece of it, so the same redundancy applies. `experiments/swin_mechanism/` found WaNet's evidence recoverable from 1 patched window by stage 3 while SIG's and Blend's never are, which is the reverse of ViT's pattern and deserves its own test.

*Prediction on our models.* On Swin, section D's visible-subset protocol should show WaNet keeping at least 0.5 of its excess ASR at 30% visible tokens, unlike ViT's 0.183, if windowed processing is what makes WaNet legible locally. The measurement ports section D to Swin. *Status.* Confirmed on ViT except WaNet, open on Swin.

**L24. Attention sinks, registers and massive activations.** *Papers.* Darcet et al. (ICLR 2024) find high-norm tokens in background patches of large, long-trained ViTs that are repurposed for global computation, and add register tokens to absorb them. Sun et al. (COLM 2024) find a few activations of enormous magnitude at fixed dimensions and tokens that act as biases, in language models and in CLIP and DINOv2 ViTs. Xiao et al. (ICLR 2024) show that language models dump attention on the first token as a sink. Jiang et al. (arXiv 2025) trace the high-norm tokens to a few "register neurons" and move them into an extra token at test time without retraining. *Claim.* Transformers create a small number of special tokens that carry global information and absorb attention, and perturbing those tokens changes the network far more than perturbing ordinary ones.

*Evidence and architectures.* DeiT-III, OpenCLIP and DINOv2 at ViT-L scale and above, where the artifacts appear, and language models for sinks and massive activations. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Our ViT-B/16 checkpoints are smaller and shortly fine-tuned, and H48 found no native sink population (0.000% of tokens above twice the median norm). The trigger instead manufactures its own high-norm token: register neurons are disjoint from the trigger's (0 of the top 10 and 0 of the top 50), and 64 spare test-time registers leave ASR at 1.000. This matters for PSBD because a sink-like trigger token is exactly the kind of token whose residual entry, spared by PSBD-TM, the class token reads. Swin has no global token to become a sink, and windowed attention limits any sink to its window.

*Prediction on our models.* If the trigger token acts as a sink, masking every token except the trigger's in blocks 9 to 12 should leave triggered BadNets predictions at least 0.95, because the class token needs only the sink. The measurement is section A's protocol with the complement set. *Status.* Confirmed that the trigger makes its own high-norm token, sink role for detection open.

**L25. LayerNorm absorbs additive noise and not masking.** *Papers.* Ba et al. (2016) define LayerNorm and note its invariance to re-scaling of a token. Brody et al. (Findings of ACL 2023) show that LayerNorm projects each token onto the hyperplane orthogonal to the all-ones vector and scales it onto a sphere. *Claim.* Normalizing by the per-token standard deviation undoes part of any perturbation that inflates that deviation, which isotropic additive noise does and zeroing a whole token does not (a zeroed token has no deviation to normalize and survivors are rescaled back).

*Evidence and architectures.* Recurrent networks for Ba et al., which predates the transformer, and a theoretical analysis with small transformer experiments for Brody et al. Neither studies perturbation at inference. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* Both architectures place LayerNorm before attention and before the MLP. This project measured the absorption: 0.179 of a Gaussian disturbance removed at the attention input and 0.128 at the MLP input, near 0 for token masking (H47, `\AbsorptionAttentionInputGaussianRemoved`). It explains why Gaussian noise reads 0.183 below token masking at the attention input and the reversal at the MLP input.

*Prediction on our models.* None outstanding for ViT. On Swin the same absorption should hold at `norm1` and `norm2` within 0.03 of ViT's shares. *Status.* Confirmed on ViT.

## Trigger geometry and frequency

**L26. Frequency content of triggers and the low-pass behavior of attention.** *Papers.* Zeng et al. (ICCV 2021) show that common triggers carry high-frequency artifacts and propose smooth, low-frequency triggers (the LF attack). Park and Kim find that self-attention acts as a low-pass filter on feature maps and convolution as a high-pass filter. Barni et al. (ICIP 2019) introduce SIG, a horizontal sinusoid applied to target-class images only. Wang et al. (BppAttack, CVPR 2022) quantize color depth with dithering and train with contrastive adversarial samples. Nguyen and Tran (WaNet, ICLR 2021) warp the image with a smooth displacement field and add a "noise mode" of randomly warped images labeled with their true class so that only the exact warp fires. *Claim.* Low-frequency, spatially stationary triggers are carried redundantly by every token and survive the low-pass mixing of attention, while triggers defined by fine spatial relations need exact geometry.

*Evidence and architectures.* Zeng et al. and the attack papers use ConvNets, Park and Kim compare ViT and ResNet on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* The ViT reading is concrete. A 32-pixel CIFAR image upsampled to 224 gives each 16 by 16 token about 2.3 original pixels on a side. SIG's sinusoid has a period of about 5 original pixels at its default frequency, so a single token sees less than half a period and reads as a gradient, and several neighboring tokens are needed to see the oscillation. That predicts SIG legible from 30% of tokens (measured 0.910) and not from 10% (0.010). BPP's quantization is a per-pixel property that every token shows in full (0.618 at 10%). WaNet's warp is a displacement between neighboring pixels, and its noise mode trains the model to reject random warps, so the model must check the exact field over a wide area. That predicts WaNet cannot be read from a random subset (0.183 at 30%) and that masking pushes its triggered and clean predictions alike toward the target (38% to 94% of clean images sent to the target when masked).

*Prediction on our models.* 2 cheap predictions follow. A structured mask that keeps whole rows of tokens, preserving SIG's horizontal oscillation, should keep SIG's excess ASR at 10% visible tokens at 0.3 or more, against 0.010 for random tokens. A per-token linear probe for warped against noise-warped images at blocks 4 to 8 should sit near 0.5 accuracy while a probe on the class token exceeds 0.9, if WaNet's trigger is a coherence check. The measurements use `TokenBlockMask` for the rows and `analysis/features.py` for per-token features. *Status.* Consistent with section D, the structured-mask and probe tests open.

**L27. Adversarial examples and backdoors.** *Papers.* Ilyas et al. (NeurIPS 2019) argue that adversarial examples exploit non-robust but predictive features, and Karayalçin et al. read triggers as such features. Weng et al. (NeurIPS 2020) find a trade-off between adversarial robustness and backdoor robustness. Niu et al. (TPAMI 2024) find adversarial examples against backdoored models land on the target, and Yin et al. (AAAI 2025) find adversarial attacks from triggered images return to the true class. *Claim.* The backdoor direction is a direction adversarial search can find, so adversarial perturbations of clean inputs activate the backdoor and adversarial perturbations of triggered inputs deactivate it.

*Evidence and architectures.* ConvNets throughout, with ViT-B/16 in Karayalçin et al.'s Section 6. *Carry-over to ViT and Swin.* It carries over to the extent the target is reachable. Our `adversarial_direction` experiment found PGD from clean images reaching the target for WaNet at 16.1% and BPP at 13.2% at 1% poisoning and for BadNets at 0.0% to 0.6%, with 0.2% on the benign model, and a strongly negative late-layer cosine to the backdoor direction from triggered images for BadNets, WaNet, BPP and LF. The attacks that PGD can activate are the ones with target attraction under PSBD-RD (L10), which says attraction and adversarial reachability are one property.

*Prediction on our models.* Across the 57 models the per-model share of PGD clean-to-target hits should correlate with the target share of shifted clean predictions under PSBD-RD at a Spearman of at least 0.5. The measurement runs `adversarial_direction` on the panel. *Status.* Partial, 1 CIFAR-100 model per attack.

## Evaluations of PSBD beyond ResNet and VGG

I found no published evaluation of PSBD on a transformer. OpenAlex lists 4 works citing the CVPR 2025 version, Shortcutprobe (ISDFS 2026), Mellivora Capensis (IEEE TDSC 2025), CipherBD (IEEE TDSC 2026) and WFR-Guard (2026). Semantic Scholar lists 9 citing the arXiv version, among them Sabolić et al. ("Seal Your Backdoor with Variational Defense", ICCV 2025) and DistScan (arXiv:2608.19088), a model-level detector for object detectors. None of the abstracts reports PSBD on a ViT or Swin. Shortcutprobe was read in full on 2026-09-29 (`literature/shortcutprobe-prasad-isdfs2026/`, untracked because the PDF is licensed). It evaluates ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 only, at 10% poisoning, as a detector of poisoned training samples, so it does not touch transformers. It fuses 2 scores with equal weights. The first is PSBD's absolute PSU under an "Active Neuron Dropout" that drops only positive activations, over 20 passes, at the rate that maximizes the clean shift ratio. The second is a "Maximum Sudden Confidence Drop" under gradient-times-activation ranked channel masking. Its threshold maximizes Youden's J, which needs the true positive rate and so reads poisoned samples, and its PSBD column is converted from Li et al.'s published rates rather than rerun. 2 of its ideas are testable on our models: activation-aware dropout as an operator, and importance-ranked masking as a deterministic partner. It is not arXiv:2505.13910, which shares the name: that paper (Zheng, Ye and Zhang, IJCAI 2025) mitigates spurious bias by probing prediction shortcuts in latent space and evaluates no backdoor detector (checked 2026-09-29). The perturbation-consistency detectors that did test transformers are TeCo (MobileViT-xs and Swin-T-Base), BaDExpert (ViT) and BAN (a 12-layer Swin, model-level). Among the related ViT analyses, Karayalçin et al. use the same ViT-B/16 architecture and BackdoorBench checkpoints and never perturb at inference.

## Contradictions and missing controls

**PSBD's own evidence for neuron bias.** The mechanism figure shows 64 of 512 feature maps of 1 BadNets ResNet-18 at 1 dropout rate, with no benign model under the same trigger and no measurement of where shifted clean predictions go on a backdoored model. The shifted-label histogram is shown for a benign model, where it concentrates on class 3, which is attraction to a default class that exists without any backdoor. On our models the default-class effect is the larger one: the largest single class takes 0.54 to 0.87 of shifted clean predictions under PSBD-TM while the target takes 0.000 to 0.428.

**Doan et al. against PSBD-TM on the sign.** Input patch dropping flags instability of patch triggers, and PSBD-TM flags their stability. Both are right, and the site decides the sign (L18). A paper that reports PSBD-TM on patch triggers should cite Doan et al. and say why the sign differs.

**Karayalçin et al. against section D on WaNet.** Their per-token legibility of stealthy triggers does not hold for WaNet on our models, while it holds for BPP. The difference could be WaNet's noise mode, which BackdoorBench and our attack both use, or checkpoint variance, since our adversarial reproduction of their WaNet cosine also failed.

**Naseer et al. against our clean fragility.** ImageNet ViTs keep 60% accuracy at 80% occlusion. Our clean predictions keep 0.11 to 0.52 of their accuracy at 30% visible tokens. The resolution and training differences under L19 are plausible and untested.

**IBD-PSC's theorem against its own detector on ViT.** The calibrated port works (0.921) while the theorem's predicted target collapse does not occur (at most 0.223 at factor 15). The detector's success needs a mechanism other than the one it states.

**SCALE-UP's theorem against BPP.** The theorem predicts consistency rising with the poison rate, and BPP's reading falls from 0.472 to 0.369. The theorem's regime of near-equal poisoned and benign counts is far from any rate we train, so the failure shows that the regime does not extend down to the rates we train and leaves the theorem itself untouched.

**TeCo's Swin result is a missing control for us.** TeCo is the only method in the family with a published Swin result, and our Swin panel has not been scored with it. Without that run a claim that PSBD-TM transfers to Swin better than its competitors has no competitor on Swin to compare against.

**A benign control is missing from 2 open tests.** The target-class prior test (P10) and the seed-ensemble test (P2) both need the benign reference of each dataset read the same way. Without it their numbers cannot be told apart from ordinary class difficulty.

\section{Where the backdoor lives in a ViT}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\Cref{sec:results} is a ranking. This section explains it by following a backdoor through a ViT, each experiment on the same backdoored models with the benign model of the same dataset, probed with the same trigger, as the control.

\subsection{A direction, written late}
\label{sec:mechanism:direction}

For a model and a layer, the backdoor direction is the mean difference between the class token's activation on a triggered image and on its clean copy, the construction Karayal\c{c}in et al. use on ViTs. \Cref{tab:direction-ablation} in \cref{app:mechanism} tests whether it is causal. Projecting the final layer's direction out of the class token takes attack success to \AblationDirectionMinAsr{} on BadNets and to at most \AblationDirectionMaxAsr{} on any of the \AblationCheckpoints{} attacks, while clean accuracy falls by at most \AblationCleanAccuracyCost{}. Projecting out a random direction of the same norm leaves attack success at \AblationRandomDirectionMinAsr{} or above, and so does zeroing the 20 coordinates the trigger moves most. The backdoor is a direction in the residual stream rather than a set of neurons, so a method that ranks coordinates, heads or channels sees only a rotated shadow of it. LF, the exception at the final layer, falls to \ErasureLfAsrAfterAllWritesMax{} once the direction is removed from every block's write to the stream. The direction stays near the benign level through the first half of the network and is written in the last third (\cref{app:mechanism}).


\subsection{Routed through attention from the trigger's own tokens}
\label{sec:mechanism:routing}

Activation patching \cite{zhang2024patching} asks where the backdoor sits at each depth. At a chosen block we overwrite a group of tokens on the triggered image with their clean values and measure how much of the clean prediction comes back (\cref{fig:patching} in \cref{app:mechanism}). For the local triggers, patching the trigger's own tokens recovers \PatchingLocalEarlyTrigger{} of the clean prediction over blocks \PatchingEarlyLayers{}, and the class token overtakes them only at block \PatchingLocalCrossover{} on average. A random group of the same size never exceeds \PatchingRandomNullMax{}. The class token's attention on the trigger's tokens rises from \RoutingBadnetClsEarly{} in blocks 1 to 4 to \RoutingBadnetClsLate{} in blocks 9 to 12 on BadNets, against \RoutingBenignClsLate{} on the benign model. For a local trigger the backdoor therefore sits in the trigger's own tokens for most of the network and reaches the class token through attention only in the last quarter. A global trigger covers every patch, so this test can only confirm that its backdoor is spread over the tokens.


\subsection{Which predictions survive each placement}
\label{sec:mechanism:placements}

A placement works when it changes clean predictions and leaves triggered ones in place. We read both shares at each placement's adaptive rate, where PSBD-TM changes \SurvivalTmClean{} of clean test predictions and PSBD-RD \SurvivalRdClean{} (\cref{fig:survival}, per attack in \cref{tab:survival}). PSBD-TM changes only \SurvivalTmBadnetATwooTriggered{} of triggered BadNets predictions, \SurvivalTmBlendTriggered{} of Blend and \SurvivalTmLfTriggered{} of LF. PSBD-RD leaves the global triggers almost as intact, changing \SurvivalRdGlobalTriggered{} of their triggered predictions against \SurvivalTmGlobalTriggered{} for PSBD-TM, but it changes \SurvivalRdBadnetATwooTriggered{} of triggered BadNets predictions. The two placements therefore part on patch triggers. BadNets reads \SurvivalTmBadnetATwooAuroc{} under PSBD-TM and \SurvivalRdBadnetATwooAuroc{} under PSBD-RD, while the global triggers read \SurvivalTmGlobalAuroc{} and \SurvivalRdGlobalAuroc{}.

\Cref{tab:survival-sites} in \cref{app:survival} asks which property of a perturbation lets a patch trigger through, on the \SurvivalSiteModels{} BadNets models. Masking whole tokens at the attention input changes \SurvivalSiteMaskAttentionInputTriggered{} of triggered predictions and the same mask on the attention branch output changes \SurvivalSiteMaskAttentionOutputTriggered{}. Applied to the residual stream after the attention addition, the same operator changes \SurvivalSiteMaskStreamTriggered{}, close to the \SurvivalSiteDropoutStreamTriggered{} of PSBD-RD. At the attention input, dropout changes \SurvivalSiteDropoutAttentionInputTriggered{}, channel masking \SurvivalSiteChannelAttentionInputTriggered{} and Gaussian noise \SurvivalSiteNoiseAttentionInputTriggered{}. All three perturb every token instead of removing some tokens whole. The perturbations that leave a patch trigger's prediction in place are the ones that leave the residual stream untouched and remove whole tokens rather than perturbing every token.


\paragraph{The causal test.}
We then perturbed only chosen tokens of the \CausalModels{} BadNets models (\cref{tab:causal-patch} in \cref{app:survival}). Masking the trigger's tokens at the attention input of every block leaves \CausalMaskAllOneTwo{} of triggered predictions on the target, and masking as many random tokens leaves \CausalMaskRandomAllOneTwo{}, so the trigger's tokens are the route. Masking them only in blocks 1 to 4 leaves \CausalMaskBlocksOneFour{}, since a token masked at one block's attention input keeps its entry in the stream and is read again by the next block, while masking them in blocks 9 to 12 leaves \CausalMaskBlocksNineOneTwo{}. Under PSBD-TM itself a triggered prediction survives \CausalTmLateNone{} of the passes in which some trigger token stays visible in every one of blocks 9 to 12 and \CausalTmLateAll{} of the passes that mask all of them in all four, which happens in only \CausalTmLateAllShare{} of passes because each block draws its own mask. Clean predictions survive \CausalTmCleanKept{} of passes. PSBD-RD fails for the opposite reason. Its dropout restricted to the trigger's positions keeps \CausalRdTriggerOnly{} of triggered predictions, as few as dropout on every position (\CausalRdAllTokens{}). As many random positions keep \CausalRdRandomSameCount{}. Dropout on every other position also breaks some (\CausalRdAllButTrigger{} kept), so the trigger's entries in the stream are the main target but not the only one.

A global trigger survives both placements because it can be read from a few tokens. With \CausalVisibleShare{} of tokens left visible in every block, Blend, BPP, LF and SIG keep \CausalVisibleGlobalTriggerMin{} to \CausalVisibleGlobalTriggerMax{} of their effect on the target while clean accuracy keeps \CausalVisibleGlobalCleanMin{} to \CausalVisibleGlobalCleanMax{}. WaNet keeps \CausalVisibleWanetTrigger{} against \CausalVisibleWanetClean{} for clean accuracy. Its warp needs most tokens, as clean content does, which is where PSBD-TM is weakest.

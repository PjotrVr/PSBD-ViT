\section{Transfer to Swin-S}
\label{sec:swin}

Swin-S \cite{liu2021swin} restricts attention to shifted windows of 7 by 7 tokens and merges patches between its four stages, so a token reaches distant tokens only gradually, through the alternating window shift within a stage and patch merging between stages. That tests whether the placement result depends on global attention. We read the same placements on \SwinCells{} backdoored Swin-S models over \SwinDatasets{} datasets, and \SwinPlacementsReported{} placements carry at least 5 of them (\cref{tab:swin-placements}).

\paragraph{The ranking transfers.}
Token masking at the attention input reads a mean AUROC of \SwinRecommendedAurocAdaptive{} over \SwinRecommendedN{} models against \SwinPublishedAurocAdaptive{} for dropout after both residual adds, a paired gain of \SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublished{} [\SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublishedLow{}, \SwinGainRecommendedMinusPublishedHigh{}], close to the \HeadlineGainAdaptiveAuroc{} on ViT. It flags \SwinRecommendedTprOneZero{} of triggered inputs at a 10\% false-positive budget against \SwinPublishedTprOneZero{}. Moving dropout to the attention input with the operator held fixed gains \SwinGainDropoutInputMinusPublished{} [\SwinGainDropoutInputMinusPublishedLow{}, \SwinGainDropoutInputMinusPublishedHigh{}], an interval containing 0, while changing the operator at that site to a token mask gains \SwinGainRecommendedMinusSameSiteDropout{}. On Swin too the attention input rewards removing whole tokens, not the site alone.

\paragraph{The attention input beats its twin on Swin.}
On ViT, token masking at the attention input ties with its twin on the attention branch output. On Swin it leads the twin by \SwinGainRecommendedMinusTwin{} [\SwinGainRecommendedMinusTwinLow{}, \SwinGainRecommendedMinusTwinHigh{}]. The lead holds on both halves of a split of the models (\cref{app:protocol}), which is why we recommend the attention input on both architectures.
